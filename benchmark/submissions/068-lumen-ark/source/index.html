Essay · 06.26

On borrowed light

How reflected daylight can turn an ordinary threshold into a room of its own.

We begin every project by watching. Light rarely enters a room in only one way: it bounces from water, stone, leaves and neighbouring walls. These secondary sources often make the deepest atmosphere. We draw them as carefully as structure.

Journal · 03.26

A wall, weathered

Learning from lime, rain and the long memory of material.

A building does not end when construction does. Its surfaces hold seasons and touch. We choose materials that gain depth through use, accepting weather as a quiet collaborator rather than a problem to conceal.

Conversation · 11.25

The generous plan

In conversation with landscape architect Liv Ravn.

Generosity is not excess. It can be a wider landing, a bench in the sun, a view held open. Small spatial permissions help people inhabit a place on their own terms.
