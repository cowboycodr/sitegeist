01 / Our approach

The world is our
workbook.

Children learn deeply when their whole selves are involved. Our mixed-age groups spend unhurried days outdoors—observing, building, wondering, and caring for a place they know well.

01

Notice closely

Attention comes first. We draw what we see, listen for patterns, and keep field notes through every season.

02

Make with purpose

Knives, clay, charcoal, cordage: real tools invite patience, capability, and the pleasure of useful work.

03

Belong together

Mixed ages make room for leadership and care. Children learn with—and become responsible to—one another.

02 / A week outside

Rhythm gives
curiosity room.

We follow water downhill, test depth and flow, and map the small lives found along the banks.

Ecology · Movement · Field notes

Hands learn the grain of green wood through carving, building, knot work, and collaborative shelter-making.

Craft · Mathematics · Collaboration

Our all-day walk makes space for stamina, surprise, storytelling, and following whatever catches our notice.

Navigation · Story · Endurance

We tend beds, gather what is ready, cook over the fire, and return scraps to the soil.

Food systems · Care · Chemistry

03 / The place

Known ground.
Endless discovery.

Our campus is a living classroom at the edge of town: oak woods, a spring-fed creek, sunny meadow, kitchen garden, and a timber barn for wet-weather making.

“By returning to the same patch of earth, children begin to see not scenery, but relationship.”
