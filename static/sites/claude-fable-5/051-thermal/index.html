The technology

Every furnace is a power plant in waiting

Roughly half of the energy industry consumes leaves the site as heat — up flues, out of kilns, off compressor jackets. Thermal intercepts that loss with three tightly coupled systems.

Capture arrays

Corrosion-proof exchanger panels bolt directly onto flue stacks and exhaust ducts, pulling usable heat from streams as cool as 120 °C without touching the process behind them.

Thermal batteries

Captured energy charges dense blocks of engineered stone to 800 °C. The store holds for days with under 1% daily loss, decoupling recovery from demand.

Return systems

On demand, the store discharges as process steam, district heating, or grid-quality electricity through an organic Rankine turbine — dispatched by the site, not the weather.

How it works

Four steps, one closed loop

  1. 01

    Audit

    We map every heat stream on site with portable sensor rigs and model the recoverable load within two weeks.

  2. 02

    Install

    Capture arrays and battery modules arrive as prefabricated skids. Typical fitting takes one maintenance window.

  3. 03

    Recover

    Waste heat charges the store continuously while production runs — no change to the upstream process.

  4. 04

    Return

    Energy flows back as steam, heat, or power exactly when the site needs it, metered to the kilowatt-hour.

Impact report — 2025

What recovered heat delivered last year

Across 38 operating sites — steel, glass, cement, and food processing — Thermal systems turned exhaust into a second energy supply.

612GWh

of heat recovered and returned — enough to power a city of 60,000 homes for a year.

184kt CO₂e

of emissions avoided by displacing gas boilers and grid peaker plants.

31%

average cut in site energy bills, with payback typically inside four years.
