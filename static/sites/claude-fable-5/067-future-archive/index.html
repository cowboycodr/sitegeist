How preservation works

Four layers between your files and oblivion

  1. 01

    Ingest & describe

    We capture bit-perfect copies, record checksums, and interview creators while the context is still alive.

  2. 02

    Replicate

    Every object lives on at least four independent media types across three legal jurisdictions. No single failure erases it.

  3. 03

    Verify forever

    Automated fixity audits re-read every byte on a rolling schedule. Silent corruption is caught, repaired, and logged.

  4. 04

    Migrate & witness

    When a format dies, we move the work forward — and keep the original plus a rendered witness copy, so nothing is lost in translation.

What we believe

Principles of a long institution

Ordinary things count

History is not only the famous files. A fan forum, a family blog, a mixtape playlist — these are the primary sources of online life.

Context is the artifact

A file without its meaning is noise. We preserve the who, when, and why alongside the bytes.

Consent outlives us

Donors set access rules — open, delayed, or sealed — and those rules are enforced by the archive, not by trust in future staff.

Plan in centuries

Endowment-funded, format-agnostic, and governed to survive its founders. The archive assumes we will be gone before it is.

For creators, estates & institutions

Preserve a collection

Bring us a body of work — a finished project, an at-risk platform export, an artist's estate. Accession takes three steps:

  1. Appraisal. An archivist reviews scope, rights, and risk with you. Most reviews finish within two weeks.
  2. Deed of deposit. You choose access terms and embargo dates; we commit to storage class and audit cadence in writing.
  3. Ingest. We take custody, publish the finding aid, and send you the first fixity report.

Reading-room desks accept deposit inquiries in person, and every regional mirror keeps open office hours on the first Friday of each month.
