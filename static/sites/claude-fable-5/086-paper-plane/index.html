Dispatch No. 28

Tbilisi: The City That Pours Slowly

Filed from Sololaki, Georgia — by a slow hand, over three weeks

The first thing Tbilisi teaches you is that a courtyard is a room. Push past any leaning door in Sololaki and the street noise drops away: laundry strung between carved wooden balconies, a fig tree nobody planted, a grandmother who nods as if she has been expecting you since spring. The city does not open all at once. It pours slowly, like the amber wine here that spends months underground in clay before anyone is allowed to taste it.

We spent the first week doing almost nothing, which in Tbilisi is a legitimate itinerary. Mornings at the sulfur baths in Abanotubani, where the domed brick roofs steam at street level and an attendant scrubs a year of airports off your back. Afternoons climbing to Narikala fortress the long way, through stairways that are half public street and half somebody's garden. Evenings at a table that keeps growing — Georgians treat a supra, the traditional feast, less like a meal and more like a slow-moving argument in favor of being alive.

"You cannot rush a toast," the tamada told us, refilling the glasses. "The wine waited eight months in the ground. You can wait three minutes for the words."

The dry bridge market sells brass irons, Soviet cameras, and single chandelier crystals, priced according to how much the seller likes your face. The Fabrika courtyard hums with people who came for a week and stayed a year. And the metro — deep, fast, thirty tetri a ride — drops you at Station Square, where the night train to Zugdidi idles like a promise.

Go in October, when the grape trucks roll in from Kakheti and the whole city smells faintly of fermentation. Book nothing in advance except patience. Tbilisi rewards the traveler who arrives with an unfinished notebook and no particular plan for filling it.

Field Notes

Stay
Sololaki or Vera — old balconies, walkable everything

Drink
Qvevri amber wine from Kakheti, by the unlabeled bottle

Abanotubani sulfur domes, private room, order the scrub

Metro to Station Square; night train onward to Zugdidi

Three weeks minimum. One is an insult.

Train Routes

Rails out of Tbilisi

Every dispatch ends at a station. These are the lines we rode, in the order we loved them.

  1. Tbilisi → Zugdidi, night train

    Dep. 21:45 · Arr. 06:15 · Second-class sleeper

    Fall asleep to the clatter leaving Station Square, wake up in Mingrelia with the Caucasus pink out the window. The samovar at the end of the carriage runs all night.

  2. Tbilisi → Batumi, Stadler day train

    Dep. 08:00 · Arr. 13:05 · Window seat, left side

    Five hours from dry Kartli hills to subtropical coast. The moment the Black Sea appears, the whole carriage leans left at once.

  3. Tbilisi → Yerevan, overnight international

    Dep. 20:20 · Arr. 06:55 · Bring your own tea

    A border crossing at 2 a.m., a stamp by flashlight, and breakfast under Mount Ararat. Slow in the best sense of the word.

  4. Borjomi branch line, for no reason at all

    Dep. whenever · Arr. eventually · Standing room fine

    A creaking two-car elektrichka into a spa-town gorge. Fill a bottle at the mineral spring, ride it straight back. That is the entire itinerary and it is enough.
