From the margin

“Every page in this issue keeps a third of itself empty. Not because we ran out of things to say, but because the distance between a line of type and the edge of the paper is where the reader gets to think. That distance is the subject this quarter — between cities and the people who left them, between an artwork and its reproduction, between what a sentence says and what it withholds.”

— Odile Ferrant, founding editor

In Issue 18

Nine pieces on nearness, absence, and the space we keep between things. Three previewed here in full-margin layout.

Criticism · 6,400 words

The Long Way Around the Museum

by Tomas Ilek

Museums keep moving the benches farther from the paintings. Ilek walks four collections in three countries to ask what curators think distance does — and finds that the farther we stand, the more we photograph and the less we look.

Begins on page 12

Memoir · 4,100 words

Dialects of the Kitchen Table

by Renata Osei-Brown

A daughter transcribes forty years of her mother's phone calls home and maps how a language thins across an ocean — which words survived, which were replaced, and the one sentence that never changed.

Begins on page 58

Reportage · 7,900 words

Eleven Time Zones, One Chess Clock

by Pável Andrade

Correspondence chess still exists, and its players still wait weeks for a single move. Andrade follows a match played by postcard since 2019 and considers what patience looked like before it became a productivity technique.
