The platform

One shared stack, every essential service

Each module is open source, self-hostable, and designed with residents first. Cities adopt what they need and contribute improvements back for everyone.

  • Forms & Permits

    Plain-language application flows for permits, licenses, and benefits. Save-and-resume, multilingual by default, and readable at a sixth-grade level.

  • Public Notices

    Structured announcements for meetings, closures, and elections — published once, delivered to web, print layouts, and accessible feeds automatically.

  • Payments & Fees

    Transparent fee schedules and receipts for utilities, fines, and registrations, with fee waivers built into the flow rather than hidden in fine print.

  • Open Data

    Budgets, service metrics, and 311 requests published as clean datasets with human-readable summaries, so oversight doesn't require a spreadsheet degree.

  • Resident Accounts

    A single, privacy-respecting sign-in across city services. Minimal data collection, clear consent, and no tracking — your city hall, not an ad network.

  • Design System

    Accessible components, tested templates, and content guidance that keep every city site consistent, fast, and WCAG 2.2 AA compliant out of the box.

How we build

Principles before pixels

  1. 01

    Accessible is the baseline

    Every module ships keyboard-navigable, screen-reader tested, and legible in bright sun on an old phone. Accessibility bugs are release blockers, not backlog items.

  2. 02

    Plain language, always

    Residents shouldn't need a lawyer to renew a dog license. Our content standards turn statute into sentences people actually understand.

  3. 03

    Open by default

    Code, roadmaps, and research are public. A city that adopts Civic Stack owns its infrastructure forever — no lock-in, no license cliff.

  4. 04

    Privacy is a public service

    We collect the minimum, retain it briefly, and never sell or share it. Trust in government starts with respecting residents' data.

In the field

Case studies

Real deployments, measured honestly — including what didn't work the first time.

Permits without the line at the counter

Marlowe County replaced a 14-page PDF building-permit packet with a guided Civic Stack form. Applications that once took residents an afternoon — and often a second trip to the counter — now finish in under ten minutes, with inspectors receiving structured data instead of scanned handwriting.

The first release missed contractors who file dozens of permits a month; a bulk-drafting feature added in month three brought them on board too.

Public notices people actually see

Ashford's legal notices lived in a newspaper section few residents read. With the Notices module, every meeting agenda and zoning change is published to the city site, large-print output, and an accessible email digest from a single entry — meeting statutory requirements while tripling real reach.

Turnout at planning meetings rose most in neighborhoods that had never appeared in the comment record before.

A two-person IT team, a full digital city hall

Pine Grove (pop. 11,400) had no budget for custom software. Using shared Civic Stack hosting and templates maintained by larger member cities, its two-person IT team launched payments, forms, and open data in a single quarter — infrastructure that would have cost six figures to procure alone.

Pine Grove now contributes its winter road-closure template back to the commons, used by 40 other small towns.
