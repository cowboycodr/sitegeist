Wander

The grounds

Forty acres of second-growth forest, one creek, one meadow, and paths that never hurry you anywhere.

  • The bathhouse

    Cedar sauna and two outdoor soaking tubs fed by the spring, open dawn to midnight.

  • Millican Creek loop

    A forty-minute walk under mossed maples, with two benches and one very good log.

  • The lodge table

    Bread at seven, soup at noon, and a long communal dinner on Saturdays. Always tea.

  • The dark meadow

    No lamps past the last boardwalk post. Blankets in the chest; the sky does the rest.

Seasonal field notes

What the forest is doing

Short dispatches from the caretakers, one per season, kept honest.

Midsummer · July

The creek runs shy

Millican has dropped to its summer voice — more murmur than rush. Thimbleberries are ripening along the loop, and the swifts have taken the lodge chimney again. Evenings ask for a light sweater, nothing more.

Spring · April

Trillium fortnight

Two weeks when the forest floor turns white with trillium and we route the loop trail around the best of it. Rain most afternoons, which is why the sauna exists.

Autumn · October

Mushroom weather

Chanterelle season. Guided foraging on Sunday mornings, and whatever comes back clean goes into the noon soup. The maples along the creek go amber first.

Winter · January

Snow-quiet

The boardwalks are swept, the stoves are stacked, and the property goes properly silent. Bring a thick book. The tubs steam hardest this time of year.

Unhurried itineraries

Ways to spend a day

Suggestions, not schedules. Skip anything; nothing minds.

The Slow Morninghalf day
  1. Bread and coffee at the lodge when the bell rings at seven.
  2. Creek loop at whatever pace the moss allows.
  3. Sauna, then the cold plunge if you are feeling brave, then the warm tub because you were.
  4. Back to the porch by noon with nothing left to do.
The Ridge Dayfull day
  1. Packed lunch from the lodge kitchen — ask the night before.
  2. Drive twenty minutes to the Threefinger trailhead; climb through fir to the overlook.
  3. Eat lunch above the valley. Watch clouds get caught on the ridge.
  4. Return for the Saturday table, or soup if it's any other day.
The Rain Dayas long as it rains
  1. Do not fight it. Move a chair to the window.
  2. Tea from the shelf; the smoky one is better than it smells.
  3. Bathhouse at dusk, when the rain on the cedar roof is loudest.
  4. Dinner by the stove. Bed early. The forest will be washed by morning.
