Why it matters

Faster. Cleaner. Fairer.

Faster

Dedicated lanes and signal priority cut the average crosstown trip from 54 to 31 minutes. Frequency is the timetable: show up and go.

43%shorter average trip

Cleaner

A fully electric fleet powered by the municipal grid removes an estimated 88,000 tonnes of CO₂ per year — the exhaust of 19,000 cars.

88k tCO₂ avoided yearly

Fairer

Level boarding at every door, audio and tactile wayfinding, fare capping, and free transfers — designed with disability and transit-equity advocates from day one.

100%step-free stations

The road ahead

From proposal to platform

  1. 2026 · Public review

    Open consultation across all nine districts, with route workshops in six languages.

  2. 2028 · Ground breaks

    Crosstown bus rapid transit opens first — dedicated lanes delivered in 20 months.

  3. 2035 · Rail arrives

    Harborline and Meridian enter service; Union Exchange becomes the city's central hub.

  4. 2050 · Full network

    All four corridors complete. Nine in ten residents live a ten-minute walk from rapid transit.

Read the proposal

The full plan, in plain language

The complete Transit 2050 proposal covers routes and station areas, funding and phasing, accessibility standards, and how public feedback shaped every corridor. Summaries below; the full document is available at every library branch and district office.

  • Funding & phasing

    A 60/25/15 split across regional, municipal, and land-value sources. No fare increases fund construction.

  • Accessibility standard

    Every station step-free, every vehicle level-boarding, every sign paired with audio and braille.

  • Street reallocation

    38 km of protected cycle lanes and 12 new pedestrian plazas built alongside the corridors.
