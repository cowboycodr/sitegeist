Field notes

From the perfumer's notebook

Every landscape is walked before it is composed. These entries travelled home in a canvas satchel, pressed between blotters.

Entry 14 — late summer

The grass here does not smell green. It smells baked, like bread crust and warm paper. Only when a stem snaps underfoot does the sap flash through — bitter, vegetal, gone in a breath. High Grass keeps that snap in its opening and lets the bake settle underneath.

Entry 22 — the storm

You smell the rain before you see it: the plain exhales all at once. Stone first, then soil, then something almost metallic riding the cold gust. I stood with blotters raised like small flags. Mineral Rain is that exhale, held.

Entry 31 — after dark

Smoke at a distance loses its violence. Two valleys of night air comb it into something soft, resinous, nearly sweet. Far Smoke is not a fire; it is the memory of one, arriving on a slow wind while the earth cools underfoot.

The atelier

Distilled slowly, in small numbered batches

Veldt Parfums works with wild-harvested botanicals gathered under permit and dried in open air. Each batch is macerated for a full season, then rested in amber glass until the landscape settles into place. Bottles are filled, waxed, and numbered by hand.
