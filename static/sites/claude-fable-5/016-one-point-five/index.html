One Point Five

Make the future measurable.

In 2015, the world agreed to hold warming to 1.5 °C above the pre-industrial average. In 2024, the planet crossed that line for a full calendar year for the first time. This observatory turns dense planetary signals into something you can read, weigh, and act on.

Section 01

Four signals, one direction

Every card below is a decade-scale trace of a planetary vital sign, drawn from the long observational records kept by the world's meteorological and space agencies. Read them together: the story is coherence, not coincidence.

Carbon dioxide

424 ppm, 2024 mean

Highest concentration in at least 800,000 years of ice-core record. Growth is still accelerating.

Surface temperature

+1.55 °C vs 1850–1900

2024 was the warmest year ever measured, and the first full year above the 1.5 °C mark.

Sea level

+105 mm since 1993

The rate of rise has roughly doubled since satellite altimetry began — now about 4.5 mm each year.

Arctic sea ice

4.28 M km², Sept. minimum

Late-summer ice cover has shrunk by roughly 40 percent since 1980, remaking an entire ocean.

Section 03

The outlook

The future is not a single number — it is a choice between emission pathways. Pick a pathway to see the central estimate of warming by 2100 and what it means on the ground.

+2.7°C by 2100

Current policies

Policies already on the books bend the curve but do not hold the line. Around +2.7 °C, most warm-water coral reefs are lost, extreme heat that was once a 1-in-50-year event arrives most summers, and hundreds of millions of people face chronic water stress.

  • ×14 more frequent extreme heat events
  • 0.6 m likely sea-level rise by 2100
  • 1-in-3 odds of an ice-free Arctic September

What the numbers agree on

Every tenth of a degree matters, in both directions. The difference between 1.5 °C and 2 °C is roughly twice the number of people exposed to severe heat, ten more centimetres of sea by 2100, and the difference between damaged reefs and vanished ones. Nothing about the physics rewards despair: warming stops within years of emissions reaching net zero.

The remaining carbon budget for a 50-percent chance of holding 1.5 °C is about 200 gigatonnes of CO₂ — five years of current emissions. Overshoot is now likely; how far, and for how long, is still being decided. That decision is the subject of the next section.

Section 04

Legible, collective action

Individual arithmetic matters most where it compounds into systems. These are the levers with the largest measured effect, ordered by scale.

  1. Move the money

    Energy transition investment passed $2 trillion in 2024 — but needs to roughly double. Where pensions, banks, and public budgets point is the single largest signal a person or institution controls.

  2. Electrify the big four

    Heating, driving, cooking, and industry heat account for most direct fossil use. Each heat pump, EV, and induction range retires a small combustion source for decades.

  3. Vote the grid

    Permitting rules, grid build-out, and carbon pricing are set by people who are elected. Climate outcomes are decided in planning meetings more often than in supermarkets.

  4. Keep the record honest

    Observatories exist because measurement is the precondition of accountability. Support open climate data, and cite it — dense signals only drive action when they stay legible.
