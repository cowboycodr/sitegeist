Restoration projects near the water you know

Every project is led by a local partner, logged in the open, and built to be joined on a weekend. Filter by the kind of work you want your hands in.

  • Habitat

    Eelgrass replanting — Harkers Sound

    Divers and shore crews are re-seeding 4.5 acres of eelgrass meadow that anchors juvenile herring and steadies the seabed against storm surge.

    Next outing: Sat mornings18 spots open

  • Cleanup

    Ghost gear sweep — Pilot Jetty

    Monthly hauls of abandoned line, pots, and net fragments from the jetty rocks — sorted, weighed, and logged before it can strangle another season.

    Next outing: First SundayGloves provided

  • Monitoring

    Tidepool census — Norling Flats

    Volunteers walk fixed transects at low tide, counting sea stars, anemones, and invasive green crab. Fifteen years of records; your count extends the line.

    Timed to tide chartsTraining included

  • Habitat

    Oyster reef build — Camas Slough

    Bagging cured shell and setting it along the slough edge to seed a living reef that filters water and blunts the wake from the ferry channel.

    Next outing: Alternate SatsAll ages welcome

  • Cleanup

    Storm-drain markers — Old Harbor

    Stenciling and tracing the drains that feed the harbor, pairing each marker with an upstream litter audit that the city now uses for street sweeping routes.

    Weekday eveningsGood first project

  • Monitoring

    Water quality relay — Broadwater Run

    A relay of sampling kits handed shore to shore each Friday, testing temperature, salinity, and dissolved oxygen at nine stations along the run.

    Fridays, 90 minutesKit lending library

Field data, kept in common

Every count, haul weight, and water sample goes into an open ledger that anyone — a harbor commission, a grad student, a curious kid — can read. No logins, no paywalls, no fine print.

  • Standardized paper-and-pencil protocols that survive wet hands and dead batteries
  • Season summaries written in plain language alongside the raw tables
  • Every dataset credits the volunteers who walked the transect
