Selected work

Projects

Three recent works, each answering the same question differently: how little can a building insist on, and how much can it give back?

Larch Fold

Private house · Calder Valley · 2024

A three-bedroom house folded into a south-facing pasture, its roofline held below the ridge behind it. Untreated larch, stone from the field walls it replaced, and a sedum roof that returns the footprint to the meadow.

Beck Retreat

Six-cabin retreat · Wharfedale · 2023

Six one-room cabins strung along a beck, each turned to its own view and none visible from another. Screw-pile foundations mean the whole retreat could be lifted away, leaving only flattened grass.

Common Ground

Public pavilion · Todmorden · 2022

An open-sided pavilion for a market town green: one generous roof, no doors, no tickets. It hosts a produce market on Saturdays, a school orchestra in June, and shelter from the rain every other day of the year.

How we work

Our approach

Four commitments shape every commission, whether it is a one-room cabin or a public square.

  1. 01

    Walk before we draw

    Every project starts with a full day on site in whatever weather arrives. We map sun paths, prevailing wind, drainage, and the routes people and animals already take. The building answers the walk.

  2. 02

    Build with what is near

    Stone from the field, timber from the estate, lime instead of cement wherever the engineering allows. Local material weathers into place instead of against it — and keeps the money in the valley.

  3. 03

    Touch the ground lightly

    Piles over slabs, retained hedgerows over cleared plots, roofs that give habitat back. We design so that removal is always possible and restoration is always cheap.

  4. 04

    Be generous in public

    Where our buildings meet the street or the footpath, they offer something: a bench, an overhang, a tap, a view kept open. A building that belongs is one its neighbours are glad exists.

From the notebook

Field notes

Short observations from recent site walks — the raw material of the practice.

12 June 2026 · Colden Clough

The sheep track crosses the plot exactly where we had sketched the entrance path. Keep the line; widen it to a metre; let the paving follow its curve rather than our first straight one.

28 May 2026 · Cragg Vale

Wind funnels down the clough hardest between three and five in the afternoon. The terrace moves to the east gable, borrowing the barn's shelter — a better view of the weir, too.

9 May 2026 · Luddenden

Two courses of the collapsed wall are still true. Salvage estimate: enough coursed stone for the whole plinth. The new work will start from stone that has been here two hundred years.

The studio

A small practice, on purpose

We are seven people in a converted weaving shed above the canal: four architects, a landscape designer, a Passivhaus-certified technologist, and a model-maker who insists every scheme is tested in card before it is tested in mud. We take on a handful of projects a year so that a partner walks every site and answers every call.

We are currently taking enquiries for projects beginning in 2027. If you have a site — or just a field and a question — we would like to walk it with you.
