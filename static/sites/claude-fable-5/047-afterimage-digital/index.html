Art beyond the frame.

An online gallery commissioning luminous interactive work from artists shaping digital culture. Exhibition 017, Persistence of Light, gathers four new commissions that linger on the retina after the screen goes dark.

Move across the light — it remembers you.

Persistence of Light · 12 June — 28 September 2026

Four commissions, always open

Each work below is live, not a recording. It renders in your browser and answers your pointer, your finger, or your keyboard. No two visits leave the same trace.

01

Phosphor Tide

Mara Ilves · generative light field

Slow waves of phosphor green cross the frame and bend toward wherever you rest. Ilves tuned the decay to match the afterglow of the cathode monitors she grew up repairing.

02

Retinal Bloom

Jun Okafor · reactive pulse system

Touch the surface and rings of magenta open like a stared-at lamp closing your eyes. Okafor calls it “a portrait of the second after you look away.”

03

Slow Ghost

Elif Andrade · delayed echo drawing

A cyan comet repeats your gestures on a delay, so the frame is always haunted by the visitor you were a moment ago. Stillness lets the ghost finally catch up.

04

Cathode Garden

Theo Marchetti · scanline flora

Stems of amber signal grow along old television scanlines and lean toward your cursor as if it were sun. Marchetti seeded the garden from static recorded off a dead channel.

Every work also settles into a still state for visitors who prefer reduced motion.

The commissioned four

Meet the artists

Afterimage commissions artists whose work only fully exists on a screen — and pays them to keep it there, maintained and open, for years.

Mara Ilves

Tallinn

Former broadcast engineer turned light artist. Ilves builds systems that decay gracefully, treating luminance as a material with memory. Phosphor Tide is her third browser-native field work.

Jun Okafor

Lagos / Rotterdam

Okafor studies perception at the edge of vision — afterimages, pressure phosphenes, the colours eyelids make. Their reactive systems have shown at festivals on four continents.

Elif Andrade

São Paulo

Andrade draws with latency. Her delayed-echo instruments turn every visitor into a duet with their own recent past, and Slow Ghost is the quietest of them yet.

Theo Marchetti

Palermo

Marchetti grows synthetic flora from broadcast noise. He archives static from decommissioned transmitters and lets it germinate into slow, phototropic gardens of signal.

How the gallery works

Commissions, not uploads

Two exhibitions a year
Each cycle commissions three to five artists to make new work that runs natively in the browser — no plugins, no downloads, no frame.
Kept alive, on purpose
Every commission stays online and maintained for a minimum of five years. Digital art dies of neglect; our endowment exists to prevent that.
Free to every visitor
No tickets, no accounts, no tracking. The gallery is funded by patrons who believe luminous work belongs to anyone with a screen.

Exhibition 018, Signal Weather, opens this winter. The four works above remain on view until then — and long after.
