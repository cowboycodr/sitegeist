Our landscapes

Living places we've made

Every project starts with what already grows there. These are gardens that feed pollinators, hold rainwater, and still leave room for a long lunch outside.

  • Corncrake Meadow

    Suburban front garden · 240 m²

    A retired lawn re-sown with yellow rattle, knapweed, and oxeye daisy. Mown paths keep the post box reachable; everything else belongs to the crickets.

  • Rain Garden at Alder Row

    Terrace courtyard · 90 m²

    Roof runoff now soaks into a planted basin of flag iris and purple loosestrife instead of the storm drain. Frogs arrived within the first spring.

  • Hedge School Orchard

    Community plot · 1,100 m²

    Heritage apples underplanted with comfrey and sweet cicely, ringed by a mixed native hedge that now hosts eleven nesting bird species.

  • Night-Scented Rooftop

    Office terrace · 320 m²

    Drought-hardy sedums and evening primrose in ultra-light substrate, planted for the moths that pollinate after the workday ends.

Planting philosophy

The garden is a habitat first

Right plant, real place

We plant what the soil, light, and rainfall can actually sustain — mostly natives, chosen for the insects and birds that co-evolved with them.

Soil is the client

No tilling, no peat, no synthetic feeds. We build living soil with mulch, leaf litter, and patience, and the plants take it from there.

Water stays home

Swales, rain gardens, and unsealed paths keep every drop on site. A garden that drinks its own rain barely needs a hose.

Untidy on purpose

Seed heads stand through winter, log piles stay put, and the leaves lie where they fall. Mess, it turns out, is where the wildlife lives.

“A garden succeeds when you stop being its only visitor.”

— Sorrel Madigan, founding horticulturist

How we work

From survey to self-sustaining

  1. 01

    Listen to the site

    We spend a season's worth of visits reading light, drainage, soil life, and what already volunteers in the cracks.

  2. 02

    Design with layers

    Canopy, shrub, perennial, and ground layer are planned together, so the garden knits closed and weeds lose their opening.

  3. 03

    Plant small, plant many

    Young plugs establish faster than showpiece specimens and cost less. Density does the design work within two summers.

  4. 04

    Hand over the keys

    We teach you the year's few real tasks — a late-winter cut, a spring edit — then step back and let the garden run itself.
