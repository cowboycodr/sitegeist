Section 01 · Supply

Where the water comes from

Three sources feed the network. Their share shifts with the seasons: the reservoir carries winter demand, while the aquifer and river intake balance summer peaks.

Millbrook Reservoir

A 92-billion-litre surface reservoir 31 km north of the city. Currently at 84% capacity, above the ten-year seasonal median of 79%.

Deepfield Aquifer

Twelve bores drawing from a confined sandstone aquifer. Extraction is capped at the independently assessed recharge rate of 13.1 GL per year.

Arden River intake

A run-of-river intake used mainly in summer. It pauses automatically whenever river flow drops below the environmental minimum of 420 ML per day.

Section 04 · Planning

The next thirty years

Demand is projected to grow 22% by 2056 while dry-year inflows decline. The long-term plan closes that gap in three stages, each triggered by measured demand rather than the calendar.

  1. Stage 1 · 2026 – 2034

    Use less, lose less

    Network-wide smart metering, pressure management, and leak reduction to 2.5%. Defers new supply by an estimated eight years at roughly a fifth of the cost.

  2. Stage 2 · trigger ≈ 2034

    Recycle for industry

    A purified recycled water scheme supplying the industrial corridor, freeing up to 9 GL per year of drinking water. Triggered when peak demand reaches 88% of firm yield.

  3. Stage 3 · trigger ≈ 2043

    Climate-independent supply

    A 25 ML-per-day desalination plant, held at design-ready status. Built only if Stages 1 and 2 leave a shortfall in the two driest planning scenarios.

The full 30-year plan, including cost ranges and the demand model, is reviewed publicly every five years. The next review opens for comment in March 2028.

Section 05 · Methodology

How these numbers are made

Every figure on this page can be traced to a named dataset, a sampling method, and a review step. If a number can't meet that bar, it doesn't appear here.

Sampling and testing

Water quality samples are collected by accredited technicians on a rotating schedule covering every supply zone at least monthly. Microbiological and chemical analysis is performed by an independent laboratory accredited to ISO/IEC 17025. Results are published quarterly, exceedances within 24 hours.

Volume and leakage figures

Supply volumes come from calibrated meters at each source and treatment plant. Leakage is calculated with the standard water-balance method: volume supplied minus metered consumption and authorised unmetered use, cross-checked against district metered area minimum night flows.

Demand projections

The 30-year demand model combines official population projections with per-capita use trends and three climate scenarios drawn from downscaled regional models. The model, its assumptions, and its error ranges are re-baselined at every five-year plan review.

Corrections

When a published figure is found to be wrong, it is corrected in place and the change is logged in a public corrections register with the original value, the corrected value, and the reason. Nothing is silently edited.
