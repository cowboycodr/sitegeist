Farm to first pour

Every lot, traced end to end

We publish the whole path for each lot — no blends of unknowns, no anonymous middle miles.

  1. 01

    Picked at the peak

    Cherries are hand-selected over three passes as each terrace ripens, usually across five weeks of harvest.

  2. 02

    Processed on the farm

    Washing, fermentation, and drying stay with the producer — and so does the premium that skill commands.

  3. 03

    Rested, then roasted

    Green coffee rests six weeks in our climate-held store before we roast in 12-kilogram batches, one profile per lot.

  4. 04

    Shipped within the week

    Bags leave the roastery no more than seven days off the drum, dated by roast day, never by guesswork.

The people behind the lots

Meet the producers

Long relationships, not spot buys. We have worked with each of these farms for at least four harvests.

  • Worknesh Alemu

    Gedeb, Ethiopia — Dawn Terrace

    Worknesh's four-hectare garden farm sits above the mist line. Her drying beds are turned every ninety minutes, and it shows in the cup.

  • Elena Rojas Quintero

    Huila, Colombia — Orchard Smoke

    Elena leads a family co-op of nine plots. Her honey process — cherries dried in their own sugars — gives the lot its baked-fruit depth.

  • Mateo Xiquín

    Acatenango, Guatemala — Hearthline

    Mateo farms volcanic slopes his grandmother terraced by hand. Slow-maturing bourbon trees give Hearthline its density and chocolate core.

  • Njeri Wambui

    Karatina, Kenya — Late Lantern

    Njeri manages her washing station's first natural-process program. Late Lantern is its third vintage, and its boldest.

How it becomes yours

The rituals

A coffee isn't finished at the roastery. These are the ways our four lots settle into a day.

The quiet first pour

Dawn Terrace, 15 grams to 250, water just off the boil. Pour in spirals and let the bloom tell you the beans are fresh.

6:40 a.m. — before anyone else wakes

The kitchen-table press

Orchard Smoke, coarse and unhurried. Four minutes steeping while the toast burns slightly, as it always does.

9:00 a.m. — the slow weekend start

The afternoon reset

Hearthline pulled short, 18 grams in, 36 out. A cortado if the day has been kind; straight if it hasn't.

2:30 p.m. — between the meetings

The last ember

Late Lantern, half-caff by choice of a small evening cup. Blackcurrant fades to molasses as the light goes.

8:00 p.m. — the day's long exhale

The season won't wait

Four lots. Twelve kilograms at a time. Roasted the week they ship, and gone when the harvest is.
