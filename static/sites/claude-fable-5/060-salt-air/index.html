Eat

The Table

One long communal table of salvaged ship planking. What the boats land in the morning is what we cook at night — the menu is written on slate around four o'clock.

A morning, roughly

  • Brown bread, still warm, with dune-honey butter
  • Smoked fish from the shed out back
  • Eggs from the Halloran farm up the lane
  • Coffee in heavy mugs, refilled without asking

An evening, depending on the boats

  • Chowder the way the harbormaster's mother made it
  • Whole grilled catch with charred lemon and sea greens
  • Roast roots and herbs from the walled garden
  • Something sweet with foraged berries, most nights

“We ate what the tide brought in, slept with the window open, and drove home two days late.”

— A guestbook entry, left in pencil

Wander

Explore the coast

Nothing here is far, and none of it is in a hurry. Borrow boots and a walking map from the basket by the door.

  1. 20 min

    The Slipway

    Down past the lobster pots to where the day boats unload. Go early and someone will hand you a mug of tea.

  2. 1 hr

    Grey Point Light

    An easy clifftop walk to the old lighthouse. Seals haul out on the rocks below from autumn through spring.

  3. Half day

    The Long Strand

    Four miles of open sand with nobody on it. We'll pack you bread, cheese, and a flask if you tell us the night before.

Read the water

Today's tide, more or less

The chalkboard in the hall keeps the real times. This is the shape of a typical day on our shore — low water is for walking, high water is for swimming off the slipway.
