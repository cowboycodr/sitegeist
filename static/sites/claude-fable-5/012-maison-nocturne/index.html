Collection XII

The Hours Unseen

Nine looks, three movements — dusk, deep night, first light. Each garment is cut once, in Paris, and never repeated.

Rue de Sévigné, Le Marais

Inside the Atelier

Twelve hands, one table, no clock on the wall. The maison works while the city sleeps.

Draped, never drawn
Every silhouette begins in cloth on the stand, not on paper. The toile is pinned by candle-warm lamplight until the shadow it casts is correct.
Hand-finished seams
Interior seams are felled and silk-bound by hand — 3,200 stitches in a single coat, each one invisible from the street.
Cloth from closed mills
Barathea, crêpe, and wool gauze are woven to order in lots too small to repeat. When a bolt ends, so does the look.
One fitting, at night
Garments are fitted between ten and two, in the light they will be worn in. Daylight flatters; the maison does not.

Private clientele

The atelier receives by appointment

Fittings are held after dusk, Tuesday through Saturday. Correspondence is answered within one turning of the moon.
