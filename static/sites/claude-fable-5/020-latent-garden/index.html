Open essays

Everything we learn, in the open

We write essays instead of press releases. Plain language, honest negative results, and figures you can replant in your own notebook.

  1. Spring2026

    Pruning as pedagogy: what dead branches teach living models

    We removed the worst 3% of Tendril's branches at every epoch and found the survivors learned faster — but only when the pruning was visible to the reward model. An argument for teaching by subtraction.

    Iris Okafor · Tomás Reyes · June Ashby

  2. Winter2026

    The mycelial gradient: cooperation without a coordinator

    A formal treatment of sugar-routing in Mycelia, showing that local fairness rules produce global specialization. Includes the four failure modes we hit first, in detail.

    Tomás Reyes · Priya Natarajan

  3. Autumn2025

    Veto power: keeping artists sovereign in co-creative loops

    Design principles from a year of Pollen sessions with twelve artists. The headline: rejection data is more valuable than acceptance data, and models should be graded on how gracefully they lose.

    June Ashby · Iris Okafor · Wren Delacroix

  4. Summer2025

    Canopy gaps: reading contradiction in latent space

    Understory reveals that a model's confusions are spatially organized. We map three "clearings" in a language model's geography and show they predict hallucination sites with 71% precision.

    Priya Natarajan · Wren Delacroix

How we grow

Principles of the garden

01 Small enough to understand

We favor models a person can hold in their head. Scale is a tool, not a destination; legibility is the destination.

02 Failure composts

Every dead end is published beside every breakthrough. Yesterday's failed run is the soil for next season's result.

03 People keep the shears

Humans hold veto power in every loop we build. A garden without a gardener is just a thicket.

04 Delight is data

If a result doesn't make someone lean closer to the screen, we ask why. Wonder is a signal worth optimizing for.
