The timeline of Earth

Select an interval to travel through the planet's four grand chapters. The scale below compresses billions of years into a single reach of your arm.

4,540 – 4,000 million years ago

Hadean — A world of fire

Earth coalesces from a disc of debris, its surface an ocean of magma. A Mars-sized body strikes the young planet and flings the Moon into orbit. As the crust cools, the first oceans condense from a steaming sky.

  • SurfaceMolten to first solid crust
  • SkySteam, CO₂, nitrogen
  • LifeNone yet — the chemistry is only rehearsing
4,000 – 2,500 million years ago

Archean — The first breath

In warm shallow seas, single cells learn to copy themselves. Cyanobacteria stack into stromatolites and begin exhaling oxygen — a slow poison that will one day remake the atmosphere and the world.

  • ContinentsFirst stable cratons
  • OceansIron-rich, green under the sun
  • LifeMicrobial mats, the oldest fossils
2,500 – 541 million years ago

Proterozoic — Oxygen and ice

Free oxygen accumulates and rusts the planet in the Great Oxidation. Complex cells with a nucleus appear. Earth freezes over more than once — a snowball world — then thaws into the dawn of animal life.

  • AirOxygen crosses a threshold
  • ClimateSnowball glaciations
  • LifeEukaryotes, then Ediacaran animals
541 million years ago – today

Phanerozoic — Visible life

The Cambrian explosion fills the seas with eyes, shells, and skeletons. Life crawls ashore, forests rise, reptiles give way to birds and mammals, and — in the last flicker of the clock — a curious ape looks up and starts asking questions.

  • LandForests, then flowers
  • GiantsDinosaurs, whales, mammoths
  • NowYou, reading this

From the vaults

Explore the collection

A handful of objects that carry deep time in their bodies. Each was buried, pressed, and preserved long enough to reach a museum case.

  • Trilobite

    521 Ma · Cambrian

    Among the first animals with complex eyes — compound lenses of pure calcite that still catch the light.

  • Ammonite

    150 Ma · Jurassic

    A coiled shell built to the golden spiral, its chambers a diving apparatus for a vanished ocean predator.

  • Seed fern

    300 Ma · Carboniferous

    Pressed leaves from the coal forests whose buried carbon still heats our homes today.

  • Shark tooth

    15 Ma · Miocene

    A serrated blade from Otodus megalodon, a shark longer than a bus and heavier than a truck.

  • Amber

    99 Ma · Cretaceous

    Hardened tree resin holding an insect mid-step, a single Cretaceous instant frozen in gold.

  • Banded strata

    2.1 Ga · Proterozoic

    Iron and silica laid down in rhythmic layers as oxygen first rusted the young oceans.

The story is still being written.
