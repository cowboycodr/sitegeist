The program

One habitat, landed whole.

Most lunar architectures assemble a base over a decade of launches. Selene lands a finished home in one flight. M01 delivers the habitat, proves 200 days of uncrewed operation, then opens the door for its first residents on M02.

H-1 systems

Solar mast
A 9-meter deployable mast catches near-continuous polar sunlight, feeding 21 kW into the habitat bus.
Pressurized dome
Two decks of living and working space behind a 40 cm regolith-ready shield wall, with a cupola facing Earth.

Mission tracker

M01 flight plan

Six phases from the pad to a powered, breathing outpost. Select a phase to read what happens and how we confirm it worked.

Launch — T+0 to T+9 min

H-1 rides a heavy-lift vehicle to a 200 km parking orbit. The habitat flies fully fueled and buttoned up, its mast and legs stowed inside a 5.4-meter fairing.

Confirmed by: clean separation telemetry and a nominal parking orbit within 2 km of target.

Cruise — Day 1 to Day 5

A translunar injection burn sends H-1 on a 4.5-day coast. Mid-course corrections keep arrival dispersion under a kilometer while the habitat runs on internal power.

Confirmed by: two correction burns closed out with residuals under 0.1 m/s.

Orbit — Day 5 to Day 8

Lunar orbit insertion places H-1 in a polar mapping orbit. Cameras re-survey the Shackleton rim site against archived terrain models before committing to descent.

Confirmed by: a landing-site go decision from the survey review board.

Descent — Day 8, 12 minutes

Powered descent is fully autonomous. Terrain-relative navigation matches crater patterns in real time; hazard avoidance retargets in the final 500 meters before a two-meter-per-second touchdown.

Confirmed by: touchdown within 40 m of the survey point, tilt under 3 degrees.

Deploy — Day 8 to Day 12

The solar mast rises, radiators unfold, and the habitat pressurizes to 70 kPa. Life support boots and begins closed-loop water and air cycling with no one aboard.

Confirmed by: 72 hours of stable pressure, power, and thermal margins.

Outpost — Day 12 to Day 212

Two hundred days of uncrewed shakedown through ten lunar nights. Every system that a crew will depend on gets exercised, faulted, and recovered from the ground before M02 brings the first four residents.

Confirmed by: full habitability certification — the door is ready to open.

Life aboard

Built to be lived in

Polar light, steady power

The Shackleton rim sees sunlight more than 80% of the year. The mast tracks it continuously, so the habitat never budgets for a two-week night on solar alone.

Water that stays home

A closed-loop recovery system reclaims better than 93% of water from air and wastewater — and the crater floor below holds ice for the years after.

Quiet where it counts

Sleep quarters sit on the deck farthest from pumps and fans, held under 45 dB. Off-world or not, a good night matters.

Earth in the window

The cupola faces home. From the rim, Earth hangs low over the horizon, never setting — a fixed point for every crew that follows.
