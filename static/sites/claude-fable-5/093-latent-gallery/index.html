The practices

Meet the artists

  • MO

    Mara Okonjo

    Lagos · flow systems

    Okonjo treats vector fields like weather. Her plotters and browsers run the same code; only the seed decides whether a piece becomes a storm or a still morning.

  • JHS

    Jun Herrera-Sato

    Osaka · lattice music

    Herrera-Sato composes grids the way others compose chords. Each cell in a lattice listens to its neighbours and blooms a fraction out of phase — order arriving late, on purpose.

  • AV

    A. Voss & ORACLE-7

    Berlin · human–machine duo

    Voss writes the constraints; a small language model they trained on lichen photography proposes the growth rules. Neither can predict what the colony will do next, which is the point.

  • CD

    Céline Duarte

    Montréal · partitions

    Duarte cuts a rectangle until it argues back. Her subdivision studies borrow their palettes from archival print misregistrations — errors promoted to composition.

Curatorial note

On emergence

Nothing in this exhibition was drawn by hand, and nothing in it was drawn twice. Each work is a small closed system — a few hundred lines of rules — released into a random seed the moment the page loads. What you see is not a reproduction of an artwork; it is the artwork, occurring.

Reseeding a piece is part of the practice, not an undo. The gallery keeps no archive. When a composition strikes you, stay with it a while: like the systems it came from, it will not come back.
