How a day here goes

Studio rituals

Nothing about clay rewards hurry. These are the habits that keep the work honest, in the order the day hands them to us.

  1. 07:00

    Wedging in silence

    Forty spiral turns per lump before the kettle is allowed to boil. The clay wakes up before we do, and it tells you — through the heels of your hands — whether it slept well.

  2. 09:30

    One wheel, one window

    Throwing happens facing the north window, where the light never argues with the form. Whatever the walls do under your fingers that morning is what the piece becomes.

  3. 14:00

    The keeping shelf

    Every trimmed pot spends a day on the keeping shelf. If it still feels true tomorrow, it earns a glaze. If not, it goes back to the reclaim bucket without ceremony.

  4. 18:00

    Closing the kiln like a letter

    We load the last firing of the week at dusk, brick the door, and write the cone number on the chalkboard. Then we leave. The kiln finishes the sentence overnight.

Field notes in mineral form

The landscapes inside every glaze

Each glaze in the studio started as a place we stood. Choose one to read where it came from and what it does in the kiln.

Riverbank

Dug from the bend below the studio after spring floods. Iron from the silt breaks red-brown over every edge, so rims and handles come out looking sun-caught even in winter light.

Cone 10 reduction · iron silt, feldspar, whiting · pools amber where it gathers

Orchard Ash

Prunings from a neighbor's apple orchard, burned clean and sieved twice. The ash runs in soft green rivulets, and no two pots wear it the same way — the orchard keeps deciding.

Cone 9–10 · apple ash, ball clay, quartz · rivulets, never a flat coat

Salt Fog

A pale grey-blue mixed the week we drove to the coast and the horizon never showed up. It hazes over texture instead of hiding it, like weather settling on a hillside.

Cone 6 oxidation · nepheline syenite, rutile, tin · satin haze, keeps throwing lines visible

Ember Field

The color of a stubble field burned in October, mixed from three failed tests that were each wrong in a useful direction. Deep russet with black speckle where the iron clusters.

Cone 10 reduction · red iron oxide, granite dust · speckles heaviest on flat shoulders

The door sticks — push twice

Visit the studio

We keep open hours so the pots can meet people before they choose them. Tea is usually on. Wet clay is usually everywhere.

Open hours
Thursday through Sunday, 10:00 to 17:00. Mondays are kiln days; you may watch the unloading from the bench by the door if you arrive quietly.
Where
The long brick outbuilding at 14 Millrace Lane, behind the old grain co-op — follow the smell of woodsmoke and wet earth down the gravel drive.
First-firing evenings
On the last Friday of each month we open the newest kiln load at 18:00. Every piece is shown once, flaws narrated, before anything goes to the shelf.
