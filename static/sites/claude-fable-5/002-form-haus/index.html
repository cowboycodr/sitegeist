Form Haus is a Copenhagen practice creating monumental spaces with a disciplined, deeply human point of view. We take away until only the necessary remains — then we let light do the rest.

Practice

Three commitments,
every commission.

  1. 01

    Reduce

    Every wall must earn its place. We begin by removing — program, ornament, noise — until the plan can be read at a glance and lived in for a lifetime.

  2. 02

    Reveal

    Concrete, timber, brick and lime plaster are shown as they are. Materials age in public; a building should be honest about how it is made.

  3. 03

    Return

    Monumental scale is only justified by human use. Thresholds, benches, handrails and daylight are designed first — the monument follows the hand.

Selected work

Projects, 2017–2026

  • Kalk Hall

    Concert hall · Nordhavn · 2024

    A limestone-clad room for 1,100 listeners. Three slots of north light rehearse the acoustics of the fjord it faces.

  • Tegl House

    Private dwelling · Dragør · 2022

    One roof, one brick, one long room to the sea. The plan is a single uninterrupted line from door to horizon.

  • Søjle Archive

    City archive · Valby · 2021

    Nine floors of paper held in a single concrete column. The shelves are the façade; the city reads its own memory from the street.

  • Havn Baths

    Public baths · Islands Brygge · 2019

    Four timber steps between the city and the water. No walls — the harbour itself is the room.

  • Lys Chapel

    Chapel · Amager · 2018

    One window, cut from eave to floor. At noon on the solstice the light stands in the nave like a column.

  • Fælled School

    Primary school · Ørestad · 2017

    Two courtyards, one for noise and one for quiet, joined by a covered walk that becomes the school's main street.

Studio profile

A small room
with a long view.

Form Haus was founded in 2014 in a former sailmaker's loft on Refshaleøen. We are twenty-two architects, model-makers and engineers working around one long table. Every project — a chapel or a concert hall — passes through the same sequence: a written brief of one page, a plaster model at 1:200, and a full-size mock-up of a single detail before any drawing is issued.

We build slowly and rarely. The practice accepts no more than three new commissions a year, and stays with each building through its first decade of use — returning to measure how the rooms are actually lived in, and folding what we learn into the next plan.
