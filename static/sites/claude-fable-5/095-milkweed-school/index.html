Learning outside · Ages 4–11

Let curiosity get muddy.

Milkweed School is a forest learning community where children explore ecology, craft, movement, and seasonal change — five days a week, in every kind of weather.

Explore the school Book an open day

We are a small school under tall trees. Children spend their days on four wooded acres — naming fungi, whittling spoons, building dens, and watching the milkweed pods split open every autumn. The forest sets the curriculum; our teachers help children read it.

What we do

Four strands, one forest

Every week braids together the same four strands. Children move between them at their own pace, guided by teachers and the season.

  • Ecology

    Pond dipping, tracking, fungus walks, and a long friendship with the monarchs who visit our milkweed meadow each fall.

  • Craft

    Green woodworking, wool dyed with goldenrod, cordage from stinging nettle, and bread baked in the clay oven the children built.

  • Movement

    Tree climbing with spotters, balance beams over the creek, long rambles, and games that leave everyone happily out of breath.

  • Seasonal change

    Sap runs, seed collecting, first-frost breakfasts, and a journal each child keeps for the whole turning of the year.

A day here

The shape of a Tuesday

No two days match, but every day keeps the same gentle rhythm.

  1. 8:30

    Morning circle

    Songs at the fire ring, a weather report from the children, and the day's invitations.

  2. 9:00

    Wander & wonder

    Small groups head to the pond, the meadow, or the big beech, following whatever the forest is doing today.

  3. 11:30

    Fire lunch

    Soup from the pot, bread from the clay oven, and stories while the coals settle.

  4. 12:30

    Craft & deep play

    Whittling, den building, wool work, or quiet time in a hammock with a field guide.

  5. 14:30

    Journals & closing circle

    Drawing the day's finds, sharing one small wonder, and a goodbye song before pickup.

Seasons

The year, as the milkweed tells it

Our calendar follows the plant we're named for — from first shoots to flying seeds.

Spring · Shoots

Milkweed spears push up through last year's leaves. We tap maples, map frog song, and plant the kitchen garden.

Summer · Blossom

Pink umbels hum with bees. Creek days, berry picking, shelter building, and our midsummer lantern feast.

Autumn · Pods

Pods split and silk flies. We tag monarchs, press apples, gather seeds, and learn the art of letting go.

Winter · Rest

Bare stalks stand in the snow. Tracking, firecraft, wool everything, and long stories in the canvas lodge.

Questions

Things parents ask

What happens when it rains?

We go outside. Children learn quickly that there is no bad weather, only unsuitable clothing — and puddles are a curriculum of their own. In lightning or dangerous wind we gather in the canvas lodge for stories and craft.

How do children learn to read and count?

Literacy and numeracy live inside everything we do: recipe doubling at the fire kitchen, tally marks in the phenology journal, story dictation, and daily read-alouds. Teachers track each child's progress against the same benchmarks an indoor school would use.

Is tree climbing safe?

We teach it the way we teach knife work: slowly, with clear rules and a trained adult spotting. Children climb only trees our teachers have assessed, only as high as they can get themselves, and never alone.

What should my child bring?

Waterproofs, layers of wool, spare socks, a water bottle, and a hearty snack. We provide tools, hot lunch, hammocks, and an unreasonable quantity of mud.
