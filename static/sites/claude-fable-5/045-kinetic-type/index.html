Typefaces

Six working families. Every release ships with static and variable builds, a motion spec sheet, and open-ended room to misuse it.

  • Gg

    Gantry

    Display · 2 axes · wght, wdth

    A load-bearing grotesk that widens under pressure. Built for headlines that arrive before the layout does.

  • Pp

    Pivot Mono

    Monospace · 1 axis · slnt

    A terminal face that leans into corners. Every glyph rotates around its own optical center.

  • Ff

    Flux Grotesk

    Text · 3 axes · wght, opsz, GRAV

    Our workhorse. The custom gravity axis lets baselines sag, snap, and settle inside motion systems.

  • Oo

    Ossature

    Display · 1 axis · BONE

    A skeletal stencil drawn from armatures, not outlines. Interpolates from wireframe to full mass.

  • Rr

    Ribbon

    Script · 2 axes · wght, TWST

    A single continuous stroke per word. Made for titles that unspool across a frame.

  • Mm

    Meridian

    Serif · 2 axes · wght, CNTR

    A high-contrast serif whose thins can vanish entirely, leaving only the gesture of the letter.

Process

We draw for the frame, not the page. Every family starts as a motion test and earns its statics later.

  1. 01

    Sketch in time

    First drafts are animated interpolations, not proofs. If a letter can’t survive the tween, it goes back to the bench.

  2. 02

    Name the axis

    Custom axes — GRAV, BONE, TWST — are designed as instruments. Each ships with a notation for choreographing it.

  3. 03

    Break it in public

    Betas go straight into the lab above. The way people misuse a face tells us where its next master belongs.
