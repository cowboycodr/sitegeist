The Collection

Four standing pieces, each in production and each individually numbered. Choose a piece to read its notes.

  • A five-foot bench read from a single ash plank so the grain runs unbroken from end to end. Wedged through-tenons, no metal.

  • A low dining chair with a steam-bent back that softens as it warms. Cherry darkens honey-deep in the first sunny year.

  • A trestle table built to be handed down. Breadboard ends let the top move with the seasons; the oak keeps its own quiet log of them.

  • A hanging cabinet with hand-cut dovetails and doors that shut with a felt-soft thud. Walnut, chosen board by board for its fire.

How a piece is made

Nothing here is hurried. A single commission moves through the bench over weeks, not hours.

  1. 01

    The tree is chosen

    We walk the woodlot with the sawyer and mark standing timber by hand, taking only what the forest can spare that year.

  2. 02

    The boards rest

    Fresh-sawn planks air-dry in the loft for two to four years until they settle into a moisture the room will keep.

  3. 03

    The joints are cut

    Mortises, tenons and dovetails are marked from the wood itself and pared to a knife line with edge tools, not jigs.

  4. 04

    The number is struck

    A final oiling, then a struck number and the maker's name go on the underside — the start of a record only time can finish.

Local timber,
named at the stump

Every board we work can be traced to a tree within forty miles of the bench. Storm-fallen, thinned or gifted from a cleared hedgerow — never shipped across an ocean to sit in a warehouse.

Because we know each tree, we can tell you where your table stood before it was a table: the field it shaded, the winters it weathered, the grain those seasons wrote into it.
