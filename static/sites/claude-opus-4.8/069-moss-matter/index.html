The collection

Three materials, one root system

Each grows from mycelium threading through a bed of spent grain, hemp hurd, or straw. Same living process — three very different finishes.

  • Corymb Packaging

    Protective mouldings grown to the exact shape of what they cradle. Shock-absorbing, water-resistant, and gone in weeks on a compost heap.

    • Mycelium + hemp hurd
    • Custom moulds

  • Lichen Textile

    A supple, leather-like sheet tanned without chrome. Breathable and warm to the touch, it ages into a soft, mottled patina.

    • Mycelium + spent grain
    • Rolled by hand

  • Understory Panels

    Acoustic wall and ceiling tiles that hush a room. Fire-retardant by nature, with a quiet, forest-floor texture.

    • Mycelium + straw
    • Press-formed

How it grows

From spore to shelf in nine days

  1. 01

    Feed

    Agricultural waste is pasteurised and packed into a mould — the scaffold our mycelium will colonise.

  2. 02

    Thread

    In warm darkness, white hyphae weave through every gap, binding the substrate into a solid, self-supporting mass.

  3. 03

    Set

    The part is de-moulded and finished to its final surface — smooth, suede, or textured to the touch.

  4. 04

    Rest

    A gentle bake stops growth for good. The material is now inert, durable, and completely home-compostable.

28 Jun 2026

The straw that wouldn't set

Batch 214 stayed spongy for three days too long. Turns out our straw held more moisture than the barley — a reminder that feedstock is a living variable, never a spec sheet.

14 Jun 2026

A textile that remembers folds

By pressing the sheet while colonisation continues, the Lichen textile learns a crease and keeps it — pleats grown in, not stitched on.

30 May 2026

Composting the packaging we shipped

A customer mailed back a used Corymb tray. Fourteen days in the studio bed and it was indistinguishable from soil. This is the whole point.
