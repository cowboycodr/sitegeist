    Voices & oral histories

    The people behind the songs, in their own words.

    “My grandmother sang this while grinding millet. I did not know it had a name until the archivists came and asked me to sing it back.”

    — Amara Diallo, Fouta Djallon highlands

    “We keep the melody exactly, but the words drift with each generation. That drift is the history — you can hear where we have been.”

    — Tomas Bălan, Maramureș valley

    “A lullaby is a map. It tells the child which mountains are home.”

    — Sarangerel Batu, Khangai range

    A living archive

    Folkways is stewarded by a small collective of ethnomusicologists, community archivists, and the singers themselves. Recordings arrive from the field on tape, phone, and memory. We clean, catalog, and — always — return a copy to the community that made them.

    Nothing here is frozen. New voices are added every season, and older entries are re-annotated as we learn more about the traditions they belong to.

    • Consented. Every recording is shared with the performer's permission.
    • Attributed. Places, names, and traditions are credited in full.
    • Open. The catalog is free to listen to and learn from, forever.
