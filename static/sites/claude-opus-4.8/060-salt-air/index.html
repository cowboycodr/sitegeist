Rooms

Weathered timber, made soft.

Every room keeps the plain bones of the old house — bare beams, lime-washed walls — with a deep bed and a window wide to the salt air.

The Dune Room

A ground-floor corner with two windows and a door to the grass. Best for a slow start and sand on the floor.

The Table

Whatever the boats bring in.

Breakfast is laid out late and lingered over. Supper is one short menu, written each afternoon around what was landed at the harbour and pulled from the garden that day.

  • Grilled mackerelgooseberry, sea herbs
  • Crab on toastbrown butter, lemon

  • Buttermilk tarthoney, thyme

Served in the timber room, or carried out to the terrace when the wind is kind.

The Coast

Out the gate and gone.

Leave the car where it is. Everything worth reaching starts at the end of the path.

The low-tide walk

Two hours of firm sand each side of the ebb, past the black rocks to the old lighthouse and back before the water returns.

The harbour

A fifteen-minute stroll to where the day boats tie up. Watch the catch come in, then follow it back to our kitchen.

Cliff meadows

A climb through thrift and wild carrot to a bench with the whole bay below and nothing else to do.

Slow mornings

Or nothing at all. A pot of coffee, an open window, and the long tidal light doing the work for you.
