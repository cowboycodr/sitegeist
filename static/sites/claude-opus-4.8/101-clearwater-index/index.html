The system

From source to tap, one stage at a time

Follow the journey of a single litre. Select a stage to see what happens, who maintains it, and where the pressure points are.

Collection & source protection

Rain and snowmelt gather in six upland reservoirs and thirteen groundwater wells. Watershed land is held in protective easement, keeping contaminants out before treatment even begins.

  • Raw water sources19
  • Protected watershed340 km²
  • Avg. reservoir level78%

Treatment

Water passes through coagulation, sedimentation, filtration, and disinfection at three plants. Continuous sensors adjust dosing so output stays within regulatory limits around the clock.

  • Treatment plants3
  • Daily capacity265 ML
  • Turbidity target< 0.3 NTU

Storage & balancing

Treated water rests in covered tanks and elevated towers that buffer demand between quiet nights and busy mornings, and hold a reserve for firefighting and outages.

  • Storage tanks27
  • Stored volume190 ML
  • Reserve margin~18 h

Distribution

Pumps and gravity move water through 2,840 km of mains. A district-metered layout isolates leaks quickly, and pressure zones keep flow steady across the hills and the valley floor.

  • Pipe network2,840 km
  • Metered districts54
  • Water loss rate11.2%

Return to the environment

After use, wastewater is reclaimed, treated to a high standard, and returned to rivers or reused for irrigation — closing the loop and protecting downstream communities.

  • Reclaimed daily148 ML
  • Reused for irrigation22%
  • Discharge compliance99.6%

Long-term planning

The next twenty years

Investment is planned in phases so rates stay predictable while the system grows more resilient to drought and demand.

  1. 2026–2029

    Resilience & renewal

    Replace the oldest 6% of mains, add pressure sensors network-wide, and complete backup power at all three treatment plants.

  2. 2030–2034

    Supply diversification

    Bring a fourth wellfield online and expand reservoir storage to hold a 30-day dry-season reserve.

  3. 2035–2039

    Reuse at scale

    Double reclaimed-water reuse so that a quarter of non-potable demand is met without drawing from raw sources.

  4. 2040+

    Carbon-neutral operations

    Match all pumping and treatment energy with local renewable generation and recovered biogas.

Methodology

How this index is built

The Clearwater Index compiles operating data into plain-language explanations. Figures are drawn from source monitoring, plant control systems, the distribution telemetry network, and the asset-management register, then reconciled monthly.

Quality readings follow standard sampling protocols and are compared against published regulatory limits. Where a figure is a target or a rolling average, we say so. This page is an educational overview and does not replace official reporting.

  • Sources reconciled monthly against audited records.
  • Limits reference current national drinking-water standards.
  • No personal or address-level data is used or displayed.
