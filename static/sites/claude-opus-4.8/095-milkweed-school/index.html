The turning year

We follow the seasons, not a bell

Every term the woodland changes, and so does what we learn. Choose a season to see what fills our days.

Spring — the waking wood

We sow milkweed and broad beans, count tadpoles in the pond dip, and learn to whittle the season's first green sticks. Frog song is our morning register.

  • Seed to seedling journals
  • Nest and burrow surveys
  • Willow weaving & mud kitchens

Summer — the humming meadow

Monarch caterpillars fatten on our milkweed while children map pollinators, build shade shelters, and cook flatbread over the fire on long golden afternoons.

  • Butterfly life-cycle watch
  • Barefoot balance & river play
  • Natural dye & charcoal drawing

Autumn — the busy harvest

Milkweed pods split and spill silk into the wind. We gather seed, press apples, catalogue fungi with careful eyes, and build dens against the coming cold.

  • Seed-saving & pod dissection
  • Fungi & leaf identification
  • Fire-lighting & shelter craft

Winter — the quiet bones

Bare branches make the wood's structure legible. We track animals in frost, read the sky, tell stories in the roundhouse, and warm cold hands on cocoa and coals.

  • Animal tracking & birdwatch
  • Star, cloud & weather lore
  • Woodwork by the fire

Rhythm of the day

A day at Milkweed

  1. 8:45

    Gathering circle

    Boots laced, hoods up. We name the weather, share the plan, and sing the wood awake.

  2. 9:30

    Deep exploration

    Small groups follow a question — a stream, a tree, a track — with a mentor close by.

  3. 12:00

    Fire & food

    We cook together, then rest, whittle, or listen to a story around the flames.

  4. 1:30

    Making & movement

    Craft, climbing, den-building, or tending the garden beds and the milkweed patch.

  5. 3:15

    Closing circle

    Muddy, tired, full. We share one thing we noticed and thank the ground we stood on.

Find the right nest

Groups grow with the child

Mixed-age bands keep siblings close and let confidence lead. Explore the school by choosing where your child belongs.

Ages 4–6

Seedlings

Wonder-led days of sensory play, songs, and the first brave puddle jumps. Everything is invitation, nothing is rushed.

  • Two or three days a week
  • 1 mentor to 5 children
  • Nap nook & snack fires
Ages 7–9

Saplings

Bigger questions, real tools, and long projects: a bridge, a dye garden, a mapped meadow census that lasts a whole term.

  • Full week outdoors
  • Knife & fire licences
  • Field-notebook literacy
Ages 10–11

Heartwood

Stewardship and self-direction. Older children lead expeditions, mentor Seedlings, and shape the wood they'll one day leave behind.

  • Overnight expeditions
  • Woodland enterprise projects
  • Transition-to-secondary support

Why milkweed

Our roots

Milkweed is unglamorous, tough, and quietly vital — the one plant a monarch cannot do without. It thrives in edges and overlooked ground. We named the school for it because that is how we see children: resilient, essential, and capable of astonishing transformation when given room to grow wild.

  • Risk that teaches

    We help children meet real challenge — height, fire, sharp tools — with judgement, not fear.

  • Slow attention

    Boredom is fertile. We protect long, unhurried stretches so deep noticing can happen.

  • Kinship with place

    Children who love a patch of ground grow up to defend the wider living world.
