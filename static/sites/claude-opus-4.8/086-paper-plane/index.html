The dispatch

A city that keeps its own time

The overnight train from Batumi arrives before the city admits it is awake. Tbilisi unfolds slowly along the Mtkvari — balconies leaning into the street like readers over a shared book, sulfur baths breathing steam into the cold, a funicular hauling the morning uphill.

We spent four days doing very little on purpose. This is the whole idea of the journal: fewer places, more attention. A guide you could fold into a coat pocket and actually use.

“The best souvenir is a route you would take again.”

Below are the notes worth keeping — the walk, the wine cellar, the bakery that opens at six, and the single hour we would not trade for a landmark.

Field notes

  • 06:10 Warm shoti bread, straight from the tone oven on Ingorokva.
  • 10:30 Cross the Dry Bridge market; take the long way through Betlemi steps.
  • 15:00 Amber wine, poured from a clay qvevri you can see through the floor.
  • 18:40 Funicular to Mtatsminda for the hour the light turns to brass.
