On the drum this week

Batch 214 landed off the cooling tray this morning. Small lots, logged by the second, shipped while they still smell like the roastery.

Drying

0:00 – 4:30

Free moisture leaves the bean. We hold the heat steady so the seed keeps up and nothing scorches on the surface.

Maillard

4:30 – 8:10

Sugars and amino acids meet. Aromas build toward toast and dried fruit; the room turns from grassy to sweet.

Development

8:10 – 10:20

After first crack we ride out the last stretch — enough to resolve acidity into structure, never so far that origin disappears.

Meet the producers

We buy from the same farms year after year. Prices are named, not hidden — character starts long before the drum.

  • Tariku Bekele

    Gedeb, Ethiopia

    Twelve hectares on a north slope. Cherries dried on raised beds and turned by hand through the cool of the afternoon.

  • Familia Ortega

    Nariño, Colombia

    Three generations high in the Andes. Slow overnight fermentation gives the Ember Deep its deep, syrupy body.

  • Nuru Cooperative

    Nyeri, Kenya

    Forty smallholders pooling a single washing station, chasing the blackcurrant snap Kenya is known for.

How we roast

No two greens want the same fire. We read each lot before we commit a curve.

  1. 01

    Sample & cup

    Every arrival is roasted small and tasted blind before it earns a place on the table.

  2. 02

    Profile

    We map charge temperature, airflow and the turn, then chase the same curve batch to batch.

  3. 03

    Rest & ship

    Beans settle three days to let carbon dioxide off-gas, then leave the roastery the same week.
