Program overview

Build once. Live beyond Earth.

Selene is engineering a self-tending lunar outpost that arrives sealed, powers itself, and proves long-duration surface living before crews follow.

Mission intent

The first flight delivers Habitat Module M01 to a carefully mapped landing ellipse in Mare Serenitatis. On touchdown, deployable radiators, solar wings, and anchor legs establish a stable power and thermal baseline without human presence on the surface.

  • ✓Autonomous landing and site survey within the first lunar day
  • ✓Closed-loop life support dry-run for 90 Earth days
  • ✓High-bandwidth relay for continuous Earth operations
  • ✓Expandable ports for logistics and crew modules

Surface habitat

M01 is a home that arrives ready.

Every system is flight-qualified for dark-side nights, abrasive dust, and multi-month autonomy before the first crew boarding window opens.

Pressure shell

Dual-wall composite hull with micrometeoroid shielding and dust-tight seals at every external interface.

Power spine

Fold-out solar arrays and regenerative fuel cells keep critical systems warm through the long lunar night.

Life loop

Air revitalization, water recovery, and thermal control run as a single supervised loop with Earth-side oversight.

Ops bay

Modular rack space for geology kits, sample cold storage, and remote science payloads staged before crew arrival.

Site anchors

Adaptive landing legs and dust skirts lock the habitat to uneven regolith and protect hatch clearances.

Growth ports

Standardized docking rings leave room for greenhouse, logistics, and crew quarters in later campaign flights.

Transfer vehicle

Serenity transfer stage

A dedicated lunar lander ferries M01 from cislunar rendezvous to the surface with precision hazard avoidance and soft-touchdown control.

Dual main engines and a throttled descent cluster deliver the Δv budget for lunar orbit insertion, deorbit, and a controlled hover above the final landing ellipse.

Terrain-relative cameras, lidar ranging, and a star tracker suite keep the vehicle aligned through Earth occultation and high-glare surface conditions.

Hazard maps update in flight so the lander can divert within a 200-meter safe zone, then settle M01 with dust-minimizing throttle profiles.
