Mycelial Attention

Dense attention is a floodlight. Mycelial attention is a network of thin hyphae: opportunistic, thrifty, and remarkably good at finding nutrients in uneven soil. We study routing schemes that activate only the pathways needed for the present context, then quietly recede.

Early results suggest sparse organic routing preserves long-range structure while leaving room for local improvisation—the same balance a forest floor keeps between stability and succession. Full lab notes include diagrams of token-to-token mycelium maps from three generative tasks.

Composting Failure Modes

When a creative model collapses into repetition or noise, the instinct is to discard the run. We treat collapse as compost: stratified traces of what the system almost understood.

This essay outlines a failure taxonomy—nutrient burn, root-bound loops, canopy occlusion—and the recovery drills we use to re-seed training. The goal is not zero failure; it is fertile failure that feeds the next bloom.

The Greenhouse Protocol

Unbounded agents are thrilling and unsafe. Fully locked agents are safe and dull. The Greenhouse Protocol is our middle climate: transparent walls, controlled weather, and doors that open only when evaluation says the ecosystem is ready.

We share the full checklist—soil parameters, pollination schedules, escape vents, and human-in-the-loop pruning—so other studios can grow surprising work without abandoning stewardship.

Photosynthetic Evaluation

Accuracy alone is a poor measure of creative intelligence. Photosynthetic evaluation asks how much ambient signal a system can convert into structured energy: coherence, novelty that still reads as intentional, and usefulness to a human collaborator.

We propose a three-leaf score—capture, transform, share—and show how it correlates with expert preference better than isolated perplexity or CLIP distance on open-ended design tasks.

The studio

A research plot for living models

Latent Garden is a small studio of researchers, artists, and systems thinkers who believe creative AI should feel cultivated rather than extracted. We publish early, simulate often, and keep our tools as open as the questions that grow them.

Our days mix paper drafts with terrarium demos: one desk might hold a branching prompt grammar; another, a soft evaluation suite that scores delight without apologizing for it.

If intelligence is to share the world with us, it should learn the ethics of gardens—patience, reciprocity, and care for what is still becoming.

“We do not train models to conquer the blank page. We tend conditions where unexpected life can take root.” — Latent Garden field charter

Practice

How we tend the work

A few standing answers for collaborators, readers, and curious passers-by.

What does “organic simulation” mean here?

We build interactive models whose dynamics borrow from growth, pollination, and succession—not as decoration, but as computational metaphors that make latent structure legible and steerable.

Are the essays peer-reviewed papers?

They are open research notes with methods and results. Some later become formal papers; all start public so critique can arrive while the soil is still soft.

Can I try the experiments myself?

The demos on this page are self-contained. Larger toolkits ship as offline packages with the same no-network philosophy as this site— everything you need grows locally.

How do you handle safety and consent?

Through the Greenhouse Protocol: scoped sandboxes, transparent logging of agent actions, and human pruning rights before any system leaves the terrarium stage.
