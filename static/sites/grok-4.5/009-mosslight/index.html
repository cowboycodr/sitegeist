Welcome

A quiet clearing in the Cascades

Moss softens every footstep. Light finds the floor in long green shafts. You arrive with a full week — and leave with a slower one.

Mosslight is a small collection of forest cabins west of the Cascade crest: timber, wool, woodstoves, and trails that begin at the porch. We keep the pace deliberate so rest can actually take root.

6 cabins on 40 acres
2 hrs from Seattle
Year-round open by season

Stay

Cabins built for deep rest

Each cabin faces a different stretch of forest. Interiors are spare on purpose: linen, cedar, a stove, and windows that hold the green.

Fernhollow

Sleeps 2 · Trail edge

A compact studio tucked under sword ferns. Perfect for two: a lofted bed, writing desk, and a south-facing window seat for rain watching.

Cedarline

Sleeps 4 · Creek view

Two rooms, a deep porch, and the sound of cold water all night. Built for families or friends who still want quiet corners.

Lookout Ridge

Sleeps 2 · Canopy

Raised on stilts above a steep green drop. Floor-to-ceiling glass, a copper tub, and sunrise over the far ridgeline.

On the land

Explore the grounds

Forty acres of second-growth forest, boardwalks over wet meadows, and a fire circle that stays lit on clear evenings.

Moss Loop

A 1.2-mile soft-path circuit through nurse logs and nurse-log gardens. Wayfinding markers are carved cedar; maps live in each cabin.

Springhouse bath

A shared sauna and cold plunge fed by a filtered spring. Towels and cedar buckets are provided; reserve a slot at the front desk.

Reading pavilion

Open-air shelter with wool throws, field guides, and a small library of Cascades natural history. No Wi-Fi by design.

Night meadow

On moonless nights, we dim porch lights for stargazing. Blankets and thermos kits wait at the trailhead after dusk.

Journal

Seasonal field notes

Observations from hosts who walk the same trails you will. Updated when the forest asks for it.

  • June field note

    Sword fern unfurling

    New croziers are open along the Moss Loop boardwalk. Watch for banana slugs after morning mist — they prefer the shaded north side.

  • May field note

    Creek running high

    Snowmelt keeps the Cedarline bank loud through early summer. Waterproof boots help on the lower spur; poles available at the shed.

  • March field note

    Quiet before green-up

    Deciduous understory is still bare, so ridge views open farther than summer allows. Bring layers — evenings drop quickly.

What the year holds

We open and pace amenities with the weather. Peak green arrives midsummer; storm season is for woodstoves and long books.

  • Spring Wildflowers, full creeks, shorter hikes.
  • Summer Long light, meadow dinners, full trail set.
  • Autumn Mushroom walks, cooler nights, harvest menus.
  • Winter Soft rain, sauna priority, limited cabins.

Unhurried

Suggested itineraries

Not schedules — gentle shapes for a few days. Keep them or leave them behind when the forest wins.

2 nights

First quiet

For travelers who need the noise to stop quickly.

  • Arrive by 4 — unpack, light the stove
  • Dusk walk on Moss Loop
  • Morning sauna and cold plunge
  • Long lunch on the porch; depart after noon
4 nights

Deep green

Time enough to stop checking the clock.

  • One full trail day with packed lunch
  • Reading pavilion afternoon
  • Night meadow under clear skies
  • Host-led mushroom or fern walk
7 nights

Seasonal dwell

Live with the weather instead of around it.

  • Cabin swap mid-week if available
  • Two free days with no planned activity
  • Cook-in kit or chef’s table night
  • Journal session with field notes staff
