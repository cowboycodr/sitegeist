Open experiments

Working prototypes, not polished products. Each entry is a hypothesis you can hold, break, or rewire.

    Room-scale null maps

    Mesh a room as absences rather than surfaces — furniture as voids, pathways as pressure. Useful when presence is the noise.

    Status: iterating Depth: 3.2 m
  • EXP-037 Perception

    Peripheral attention nets

    Models trained only on edge vision. They notice hands, weather, and exits — never the thing you were told to look at.

    Status: open F1 edge: 0.91
  • EXP-052 Interface

    Haptic grammar tablets

    A tactile alphabet for operators who cannot look up. Pressure, drag, and double-tap form sentences machines can answer.

    Status: bench Lexemes: 48
  • EXP-029 Spatial

    Lag-aware volumetric UI

    Interfaces that announce their own delay. Depth layers shift when the stack is late so trust stays legible.

  • EXP-061 Perception

    Uncertainty chroma field

02 — Research domains

Where we probe

Three overlapping labs share one bench. The interesting work always happens on the seams.

Spatial computing

Rooms as addressable media. We map occlusion, latency, and social distance as first-class interface materials — not afterthoughts.

  • Volumetric layout systems
  • Presence-aware lighting stacks
  • Shared null-space calendars

Machine perception

Sensors that admit uncertainty. We train for periphery, occlusion, and social glance — the parts of seeing that dashboards discard.

  • Edge-only attention models
  • Confidence as chromatic signal
  • Multi-agent gaze arbitration

Strange interfaces

Controls that feel slightly wrong on purpose. Haptics, refusal states, and soft failure modes that keep humans in the loop.

  • Tactile operator grammars
  • Ambiguity-preserving gestures
  • Interfaces that document lag

03 — Field notes

Manifesto for uncertain futures

We build instruments, not oracles. Every prototype at NULL is a question you can run with your hands. If a system cannot show its doubt, it does not ship to the bench.

  1. 01

    Prefer legible failure

    When the model is wrong, the interface should look wrong — not confident.

  2. 02

    Treat space as data

    Depth, occlusion, and distance are inputs. Flat screens are a temporary constraint.

  3. 03

    Keep humans interruptible

    Automation that cannot be paused mid-gesture is a hazard, not a feature.

  4. 04

    Publish the residual

    What the system cannot explain belongs on the surface, not in a footnote.

  5. 05

    Delight is a signal

    Strange joy is how we notice when an interface has become too sterile to trust.
