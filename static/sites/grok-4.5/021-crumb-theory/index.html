Obsessive pastry notes

What we're testing on the bench

Crumb Theory is a working kitchen, not a fixed menu. We write down the variables—hydration, folds, rest times—so tomorrow's bake is a little more honest.

These notes are for the curious: regulars who ask why the croissant cracks a certain way, and bakers who care about the same small things we do.

Butter temperature is the whole plot

July 10

Lamination fails in the quiet places. Butter at 58°F rolls clean; at 64°F it starts to smear and the layers fuse. We keep a cold marble block and a kitchen timer that only exists for folds.

This week's croissants used a 15-minute rest between single folds instead of 20. Edge seal improved; oven spring stayed tall. Keeping it.

Whole wheat wants water and patience

July 7

The country levain jumped to 78% hydration with a 30-minute autolyse. Bran softens, gluten catches up, and the crumb opens without going gummy.

Next trial: mill a coarser cut of the same wheat and compare crust color after a 24-hour cold proof. Notes go on the flour bag, not the whiteboard.

Frangipane should taste like almond, not paste

July 3

We lightened the cream with whipped egg white and a pinch of salt, then stopped short of full bake so the center stays custardy under the fruit.

Strawberry glaze now reduces with a splash of red wine vinegar—brightness against the butter, not more sugar. Regulars noticed before we said anything.
