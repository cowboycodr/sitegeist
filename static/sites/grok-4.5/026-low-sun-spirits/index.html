How we work

Patient heat, deliberate rest

We distill when the valley cools — late afternoon into night — so volatiles stay soft and the copper still can be read by hand. Every run is logged by temperature, wind, and the scent of the wash.

  1. Forage & mill Botanicals cut at first light or last light, never midday heat.
  2. Slow fermentation Open coolers and native yeasts shape each wash over days, not hours.
  3. Cut by nose Hearts selected by aroma and texture — instruments only confirm the call.
  4. Rest in glass Spirit rests until the edges settle and the desert notes open fully.

Single 250-liter copper pot. Two operators per run. No continuous still, no industrial shortcuts.

Field notes

What the desert gives back

We work with plants that already know scarcity — resinous, aromatic, and precise. Nothing ornamental. Everything functional.

Creosote

Rain-woken resin — the scent of wet desert stone after a rare storm. Bright, green, unforgettable.

Desert juniper

High-elevation berries with a cooler pitch than coastal gin stock — pine, dust, and blue dusk.

Mesquite

Bean pods milled for caramel depth and a soft smoke that never overpowers the still cut.

Agave heart

Slow-roasted piñas from valley floor plants — earthy sweetness that anchors Sandglass Blanco.
