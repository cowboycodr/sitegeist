Design philosophy

Motion first, then the metal

Most industrial arms start as structures and get programmed later. We reverse that. Joint layouts, cable routing, and gearing are chosen so natural arcs, soft landings, and recoverable grasps are easy—not bolted on as afterthoughts.

The result is hardware that behaves like a skilled colleague: deliberate near people, decisive in free space, and honest about what it cannot reach.

  • Whole-body compliance tuning per application
  • Shared tool bus across the product family
  • Open skill export for plant-level orchestration

Four principles of useful movement

  1. Continuity Paths should read as one gesture, not a chain of poses.
  2. Compliance Contact is expected; force limits are first-class controls.
  3. Clarity Status, intent, and stop modes are visible to nearby people.
  4. Composition Skills combine; cells scale without rewriting the stack.

Safer work

Built for shared floors

Safer work is not a mode you toggle at the end of integration. It is encoded in speed limits, sensing, and how the machine announces what it will do next.

Layered protection

Power and force limiting, monitored stops, and zone-aware velocity caps work together so a single sensor fault does not become a single point of failure.

Readable intent

Status rings, pendant cues, and audible state changes give people near the cell a continuous sense of whether the arm is idle, approaching, or holding force.

Audit-ready logs

Every intervention, mode change, and force event is timestamped and exportable for plant safety reviews and continuous improvement.

Aligned with industrial practice. Kinetic Foundry systems are designed against ISO 10218 and collaborative application guidance so integrators can complete risk assessments with fewer surprises.
