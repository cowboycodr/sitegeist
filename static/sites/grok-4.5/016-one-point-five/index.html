The guardrail

1.5 °C is not a slogan. It is a budget.

Paris-aligned science frames 1.5 °C as the line where risks to coral reefs, ice sheets, food systems, and coastal cities rise nonlinearly. Crossing it is not a single moment — it is a sequence of exceedance years that can still be shortened by rapid cuts.

  • Every 0.1 °C avoided reduces exposure for hundreds of millions of people.
  • Remaining carbon budget depends on non-CO₂ forcers and Earth-system feedbacks.
  • Overshoot pathways demand both deep cuts and durable carbon removal.

Seasonal outlook

What the next decade can still rewrite

01

Near-term heat persistence

Multi-year averages are already brushing the 1.5 °C band in some datasets. Single hot years will become more common even as the long-term mean lags slightly behind.

02

Ocean memory

Heat already stored below the surface commits us to further sea-level rise and marine heatwaves. Mitigation still shapes how far that commitment grows.

03

Action compounding

Clean power, efficient buildings, electrified transport, and methane abatement stack. Delayed starts shrink the remaining option space faster than most models feel intuitive.

04

Collective readout

One Point Five exists so dense Earth-system data stays public, comparable, and actionable — not locked in specialist silos.
