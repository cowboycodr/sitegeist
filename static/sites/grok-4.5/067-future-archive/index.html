Why we exist

The web forgets faster than we do

Links rot. Formats expire. Platforms vanish overnight. Future Archive is a public-minded preservation layer for born-digital culture—built so that artworks, community records, and civic memory remain legible long after their original hosts are gone.

  • Independence first. Collections are stored outside any single platform’s lifecycle.
  • Open by design. Metadata, provenance, and access policies travel with every object.
  • Century-scale care. Refresh cycles, checksums, and format migration are routine—not emergencies.

What we keep

Collections built for permanence

We accept materials that matter to cultural continuity—not just what is popular today.

Digital artworks

Generative pieces, interactive installations, net art, and time-based media packaged with runtime notes and exhibition history.

Cultural records

Oral histories, community newsletters, festival archives, and vernacular media that rarely enter institutional stacks.

Fragile online history

Threaded conversations, early web sites, fan archives, and civic documentation captured before platforms rewrite or erase them.

How preservation works

From intake to sealed vault

  1. 01

    Capture & inventory

    Objects are bagged with fixity hashes, original filenames, capture dates, and contributor-attested context—so nothing arrives as a mystery file.

  2. 02

    Normalize & document

    We prefer open formats, record dependencies, and write plain-language runbooks so future readers can open works without the original machine.

  3. 03

    Replicate & seal

    Copies land in geographically separate vaults with continuous checksum verification and scheduled media refresh.

  4. 04

    Access with care

    Public metadata stays searchable; sensitive materials follow donor agreements. Access is durable, not viral by default.
