Three priorities

Faster. Cleaner. Fair for every neighborhood.

Transit 2050 is not a single megaproject. It is a coordinated network of service, vehicles, and streets designed so mobility is a public good—not a privilege of geography or income.

Faster trips

Dedicated lanes, signal priority, and all-door boarding shrink wait and ride times across the rapid network—especially on the corridors people use most for work and care.

18 min saved on average peak commute

Cleaner air

A fully electrified fleet, quieter streets, and greener station plazas cut transport emissions while making walking to transit safer and more pleasant.

−62% transport CO₂ by 2050

Equitable access

Service frequency, night routes, and fare policy are planned with communities historically under-served—so time, cost, and reliability improve where gaps are largest.

95% of residents within 10 min walk

Path to 2050

Build the network in phases people can feel.

Early wins focus on service reliability and bus priority; later phases open new rail and close the last equity gaps.

  1. 2026 – 2030

    Foundation & frequency

    All-door boarding, signal priority on ten busiest corridors, and a unified fare system. Residents see shorter waits before major construction breaks ground.

  2. 2031 – 2038

    Lines A & B open

    Waterfront Express and Cross-Town Spine launch with electric fleet expansion. First major neighborhood stations double as community cooling and air-quality hubs.

  3. 2039 – 2045

    Green Loop & job access

    Orbital Line C connects campuses, hospitals, and industrial centers so workers skip forced downtown transfers during peak hours.

  4. 2046 – 2050

    Full equity network

    Line D night service, last-mile bike + micro-mobility docks, and citywide coverage so 95% of residents live within a ten-minute walk of frequent transit.

Read the proposal

A mobility plan written in public, for the public.

Transit 2050 is a living civic proposal: transparent goals, open maps, and measurable equity targets. Every corridor decision is paired with service standards residents can hold agencies to.

  • 01
    Service before spectacle Frequency and reliability first—stations and lines only where they unlock daily access.
  • 02
    Clean by design Zero-emission vehicles, quieter corridors, and greener station streets as standard—not add-ons.
  • 03
    Equity is a metric Invest first where travel time, cost, and safety gaps are largest; report progress by neighborhood.
  • 04
    One network, one ticket Seamless transfers, night coverage, and fares that treat the whole city as a single system.
