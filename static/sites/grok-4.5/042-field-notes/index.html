How we work

Landscape first, architecture second.

We begin with soil, wind, light, and the patterns of use already present. Buildings arrive as careful responses—not statements imposed on the land.

01

Read the ground

Contours, water, vegetation, and microclimate set the brief before any room is drawn. Site notes become the first drawings.

02

Build lightly

Compact footprints, durable materials, and passive strategies reduce impact while deepening comfort across seasons.

03

Make room for others

Homes, retreats, and public rooms share a common aim: generous thresholds, shared edges, and spaces that welcome lingering.

Architecture is a form of listening.

We measure success in how easily a building settles into its field—how doors open to morning air, how rooms hold community, how materials weather with grace.

From brief to building

A clear process, patiently paced

Collaboration is deliberate. We keep teams small, decisions legible, and drawings honest about what the land can hold.

  1. Fieldwork

    Walks, soil notes, light studies, and conversations with those who know the place best.

  2. Framework

    A site strategy and massing that protect what matters—views, habitat, access, and quiet.

  3. Craft

    Detail design with builders and makers, refining structure, envelope, and material joints.

  4. Stewardship

    Construction support and post-occupancy visits so the project thrives through its first seasons.

The practice

A studio calibrated to landscape

Field Notes is an architecture studio based in the Pacific Northwest. We partner with landowners, nonprofits, and public agencies who want buildings that feel inevitable in their setting—durable, hospitable, and low in impact.
