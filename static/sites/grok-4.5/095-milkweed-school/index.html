Who we are

A school that treats the woods as classroom

At Milkweed, children learn by noticing: the curl of a fern, the weight of wet clay, the way wind changes a story told around a circle of stumps. Our educators are naturalists, makers, and guides — not lecturers at a whiteboard.

We keep groups small so every child has room to lead, lag, ask odd questions, and try again. Mud, scrapes, and quiet observation are all part of the curriculum.

“We don’t rush childhood past the forest floor.”

— Founding educators, Milkweed School

  • Rooted attention

    Long stretches outdoors build focus that screens cannot fake.

  • Seasonal rhythm

    Learning follows weather, light, migration, and harvest — not just a clock.

  • Shared craft

    Making, mending, and storytelling sit beside science and play.

Explore the school

Four living pathways

Every week we braid ecology, craft, movement, and seasonal change into outdoor days that grow with the child.

Ecology

Tracks, ponds, soil cores, and species journals. Children learn to ask what lives here — and how we share the land with it.

  • Habitat mapping
  • Water & soil studies
  • Wildlife observation

Craft

Whittling, cordage, clay, dyes from plants, and shelters built with found materials. Skill grows through real tools and patient practice.

  • Wood & fiber work
  • Natural color & clay
  • Shelter building

Movement

Balance beams of fallen logs, trail games, climbing, and free roaming. Bodies learn the land as minds map it.

  • Trail navigation
  • Risk-aware play
  • Strength & balance

Seasonal change

From first frost to seed-head autumn, the year itself is the syllabus — festivals, food, weather lore, and waiting.

  • Phenology journals
  • Harvest & rest days
  • Story & ceremony

The year outside

Seasons as curriculum

We return to the same places through the year so children see change, not just novelty.

Spring

Bud watch, amphibian choruses, soil warmth, and first forage walks after thaw.

Summer

Long light, creek days, insect studies, shade crafts, and overnight story circles.

Autumn

Seed collecting, migration maps, dye pots, and gratitude feasts under turning leaves.

Winter

Track reading on snow, fire skills, stillness practices, and indoor-outdoor hybrid days.

A day outside

From circle to trail and back again

Days are predictable in shape so children feel safe — and flexible in content so weather and wonder can lead.

  1. 9:00

    Opening circle

    Names, weather check, one question for the day, and a shared snack.

  2. 9:30

    Trail & focus work

    Pathway projects: mapping a microhabitat, cordage, or a movement challenge.

  3. 11:30

    Free range

    Child-led play in known bounds — dens, streams, climbing, quiet reading under trees.

  4. 12:30

    Lunch fire

    Warm food, stories, tool care, and rest. Then a second craft or ecology block.

  5. 2:30

    Closing gratitude

    What we noticed, what we made, what we leave for next time. Hands washed, boots stomped.

Ages 4–11

Mixed-age forest circles

Younger and older learners share trails so mentoring happens naturally — with adult guides never far.

Seed circle

Ages 4–6

Shorter trails, sensory play, songs, and lots of time at the mud kitchen and log pile.

Stem circle

Ages 7–9

Deeper projects, real tools with coaching, multi-day builds, and field journals that grow thick.

Canopy circle

Ages 10–11

Leadership on the trail, independent inquiry, mentoring younger peers, and community service on the land.

Practical notes

What to wear

Layers, waterproof boots, a spare pair of socks, and clothes that can take paint, clay, and burrs. We keep loaner mittens for cold snaps.

Safety & ratios

Trained outdoor educators, clear site boundaries, first-aid kits on every trail, and ratios that never stretch beyond small-circle care.

Enrollment

Part-time forest days and full outdoor weeks. Priority for siblings and returning families. Open days are the best first step.
