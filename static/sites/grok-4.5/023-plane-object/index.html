The Plane System

One grammar. Infinite rooms.

Every Plane Object is a combination of horizontal planes, vertical panels, and a shared joint language. Change span, height, or finish—never start over.

Horizontal planes

Tabletops, shelves, and seats cut from continuous grain. Same thickness, three standard depths.

Vertical panels

Legs, risers, and backs that lock into place without glue. Swap height without replacing the surface.

Shared joints

Three hardware types across the entire catalog. Tools optional—most pieces hand-tighten in minutes.

Honest materials

Nothing veneered over. Nothing sealed in mystery.

We show the grain, the edge, and the joint. Solid ash and oak for load-bearing planes. Birch plywood for cabinets. Linoleum and wool where soft contact matters. Finishes are oil and hardwax—repairable with a cloth, not a specialist.

  • European ash & oak FSC-certified, kiln-dried, open-pore oil.
  • Birch plywood Void-free cores with exposed edge banding option.
  • Natural linoleum Quiet desktop surfaces in five muted tones.
  • Powder-coated steel Hardware in graphite, sand, and oxide red.

Flat-pack ingenuity

Ships flat. Lives dimensional.

Every piece arrives as labeled planes and a single pouch of shared hardware. Illustrated steps stay in the room—no app required.

  1. 01

    Unbox the planes

    Parts are nested by size. Protective felt stays on edges until the final wipe-down.

  2. 02

    Seat the joints

    Align dowel marks, drop in steel inserts, and hand-tighten. Torque tools are optional insurance.

  3. 03

    Level and live

    Adjustable feet settle uneven floors. When the room changes, reverse the steps and reconfigure.
