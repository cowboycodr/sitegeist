How it works

Three gentle loops that hold the week

Kintsu is not another feed. It is a rhythm: notice what is true, practice what helps, and carry insight into care with someone you trust.

  1. 1

    Check in honestly

    Name mood and energy in under a minute. Optional notes stay on your device—no account required to begin.

  2. 2

    Use a small practice

    Pick a grounding, boundary, or recovery tool sized for real life—not a marathon worksheet when you are already tired.

  3. 3

    Bridge to therapy

    Turn patterns into language you can share with a clinician, coach, or support person when the next appointment arrives.

Guided practices

Try one practice now

Expand a practice to see the steps. Each is designed to be finishable even on a low-energy day.

  • 2 min

    Box breath

    Even counts to settle the nervous system before a meeting or after scrolling.

    Inhale for four. Hold for four. Exhale for four. Hold for four. Repeat four rounds. If counting feels tight, shorten to three. Stop if dizzy and return to natural breathing.
  • 4 min

    Values check

    Reconnect to one value so decisions feel less like survival and more like direction.

    Name one value that matters this week (care, honesty, rest, courage). Write one tiny action that expresses it in the next twenty-four hours. Circle anything that is already enough.
  • 5 min

    Repair letter

    Draft what you wish you could say—without sending it—when shame or conflict is loud.

    Address yourself or another person. Write what happened, what you felt, and what you need next. End with one sentence of care. Decide later whether any part is worth sharing with a trusted person.
