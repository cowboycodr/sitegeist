The studio

A light-filled room facing the water

Sonnet Ceramics began as a single wheel beside a north window in a converted boathouse. Today the practice is still small: one maker, one kiln, and enough shelves for a season of work.

Clay is mixed in the early hours when the light is cool. Glazes are tested against the gray-green of the harbor and the warm plaster of the walls. Nothing is rushed past the point where the form can still speak softly.

  • Local stoneware Body clay sourced within the region for strength and a quiet iron speck.
  • Oxidation firing Electric kiln to cone 6—clean surfaces, reliable food-safe glazes.
  • Small editions Twelve to twenty-four of each form, then the mold of habit is set aside.

From clay to table

A measured making

Each object moves through the same patient sequence. There are no shortcuts that improve a rim or a glaze line—only time and attention.

  1. 01

    Throw

    Wet clay is centered and raised on the wheel until wall, foot, and lip agree.

  2. 02

    Trim

    Leather-hard pieces are refined: feet undercut, rims softened, weight balanced.

  3. 03

    Bisque

    A first firing hardens the form so it can take glaze without dissolving.

  4. 04

    Glaze & fire

    Surfaces are dipped or brushed, then fired to cone 6 for durable, quiet color.
