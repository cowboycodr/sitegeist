Studio philosophy

Imperfect vessels, held with intention

We throw slowly so the clay can answer back. Fingerprints stay. Asymmetry is welcome. Nothing here pretends to be machine-made.

Soft Earth Studio began with a single kick-wheel and a desire to make objects that feel alive in the hand—cups that warm with use, bowls that remember the meal, jars that hold more than storage.

Every form passes through the same quiet rituals: wedging, centering, trimming, bisque, glaze, and a final firing where landscapes settle into the surface. No two pieces leave the kiln the same.

We work in small editions and one-of-a-kind vessels. When a batch is finished, it is finished. The next clay cycle will write its own story.

Morning wedge

Clay is kneaded until air leaves and the body softens—an unhurried start that sets the rhythm of the day.

Wheel stillness

Forms rise with light pressure and wet hands. We stop when the silhouette feels honest, not when it looks perfect.

Glaze weather

Each dip and pour is a small climate: misted rims, pooled feet, and mineral skies that only fire can finish.

Surface & fire

Landscapes held inside every glaze

Minerals, ash, and heat write weather onto clay. We mix small glaze batches so each kiln load keeps a private climate.

  • River ash

    Local hardwood ash yields a soft olive sheen that breaks pale over rims and edges.

  • Clay blush

    Iron-rich slips flash warm where the flame touches longest—never quite the same twice.

  • Tide glass

    A thin celadon that pools cool in the belly of a cup and thins to a whisper on the lip.

  • Umber earth

    Matte, dry, and grounded—the color of turned soil after rain.
