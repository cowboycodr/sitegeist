Curatorial note

Looking leaves a stain

An afterimage is not a memory of what was seen. It is the eye continuing to work after the object has gone. — Founding note, Afterimage Archive, 2018

Between 1968 and 1999, experimental photographers treated the darkroom as a laboratory rather than a service bureau. Many of their plates never entered museum collections: they were filed as tests, discarded after critiques, or published only in photocopied zines.

Afterimage Archive preserves those residuals—solarizations, chemigrams, failed shutters, forced processes— without correcting them into “clean” scans. The catalogue favors process evidence over market pedigree. What remains after looking is often the more honest image.

The public holdings now number 947 accessioned plates. The nine works on this page are representative entries from the open catalogue; further records are released seasonally as rights and conservation allow.

Process vocabulary

How the image arrives

The archive indexes works by process as carefully as by maker. These four vocabularies recur across the collecting window.

Solarization

Re-exposure during development reverses selected tones, leaving a metallic edge between light and dark.

Chemigram

Painted resists and alternating baths draw without a camera; each print is unique and unrepeatable.

Cross-process

Film developed in chemistry meant for another stock shifts color into deliberate delay and cast.

Photogram

Objects or instruments meet paper directly—shadows without lenses, traces without a frame of view.

Collecting window

Thirty-one years of residue

The archive’s temporal bounds are deliberate: from the late-sixties process revival through the last decade of widespread analog experiment before digital dominance.

  1. 1968–74

    Darkroom as laboratory Renewed interest in nineteenth-century processes and solarization. Student studios produce work later filed as “not for exhibition.”

  2. 1975–82

    Duration and failure Enlarger burns, double horizons, and deliberate mechanical error enter critical conversation—then fall out of it.

  3. 1983–89

    Color as instrument Cross-processing and instrument photograms treat color temperature and phosphor as subject matter.

  4. 1990–99

    Last analog residuals Chemigrams, flare studies, and shutter ghosts close the window. After 1999 the archive stops accessioning new-made work.
