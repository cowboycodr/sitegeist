Where the water comes from

Maintenance

Keeping pipes, pumps, and plants reliable

Reliability is scheduled work—not only emergency response. The program balances risk, age, and customer impact across the asset portfolio.

Ongoing

Valve exercising & hydrant checks

Critical valves are exercised on a multi-year cycle so isolation works when a break occurs. Hydrants are flushed and flow-tested for fire readiness.

Spring

Main flushing window

Controlled unidirectional flushing clears sediment from older cast-iron corridors before summer demand peaks. Temporary discoloration may appear near work zones.

Year-round

Leak detection routes

Acoustic surveys and district night-flow analysis target buried leaks. Fast repair reduces non-revenue water and protects pavement and foundations.

Capital

Priority main replacement

Cast iron and asbestos-cement segments with break history move to the top of the multi-year replacement queue, coordinated with street resurfacing calendars.

Methodology

How Clearwater Index is built

Numbers on this site come from operational systems, certified laboratory results, and the adopted capital program—not marketing estimates.

01

Operational telemetry

Flow, pressure, tank levels, and plant production export nightly from SCADA historians. Daily aggregates power the system explorer metrics.

02

Certified laboratory results

Compliance samples follow EPA and state protocols. Only validated results that pass quality-control checks enter the public quality tables.

03

Asset & work-order records

Maintenance narratives draw from GIS pipe inventories, break histories, and completed work orders so published programs match field practice.

04

Board-adopted plans

Long-term figures align with the utility’s adopted master plan and capital improvement program, updated after public hearings and engineering review.

Questions

Common questions from residents

Short answers about taste, pressure, and how to interpret the numbers you see here.

Why does my water sometimes taste like chlorine?

A disinfectant residual keeps water safe as it travels through miles of pipe. Taste can strengthen after main repairs or seasonal source shifts. Letting water stand briefly or chilling it usually reduces the odor.

What does cloudy water mean?

Fine air bubbles often make cold water look milky; the glass clears from the bottom up within a minute or two. Persistent brown or yellow color after flushing can signal sediment from a nearby main break—check local advisories.

How current are the statistics?

Capacity and reliability figures refresh with the annual system report. Quality tables show the latest completed compliance year. Emergency notices supersede routine data when posted.

Can I use this data for research or journalism?

Yes. Attribute Clearwater Index and note the reporting year. Methodology details above explain source systems so findings can be reproduced from primary utility records.
