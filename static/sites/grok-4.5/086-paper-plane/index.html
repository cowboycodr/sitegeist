The dispatch

Tbilisi, written in half-light

Arrive by overnight train if you can. The city greets you with sulfur steam, laundry lines, and a river that cuts the map like a deliberate sentence.

On the first morning I walked from Freedom Square toward the baths with no plan except to follow the sound of water. The streets of Old Tbilisi do not hurry you; they lean. Balconies overhang the pavement like open notebooks. Cats claim the warm stone. A baker hands you bread still soft enough to leave fingerprints.

Slow travel here is not a luxury—it is the only honest pace. Climb to Narikala when the light is low and the fortress stones hold the day’s heat. Look down on the Mtkvari: bridges like stitches, cable cars drawing quiet diagonals, church domes catching the last gold. Write nothing for an hour. Then write everything.

“The wine was poured before anyone asked what year it was. The table stayed open until the stories ran out.”

— Field notebook, Day 3 · Mtatsminda

Eat where the menu is short and the host still remembers who sat at the corner table last week. Order khinkali carefully; fold the dough the way you are shown. Walk the dry bridge flea market for Soviet cameras and enamel pins you will never need. Cross to the left bank at dusk when the Peace Bridge lights begin their soft geometry.

This is not a city that performs for visitors. It holds its evenings close— in courtyard wines, in sulfur rooms, in conversations that outlast the second bottle. Paper Plane’s rule for Tbilisi: stay long enough that a street starts recognizing you.

Train & foot

Routes worth the ticket

Three paths through and beyond the city—timed for people who prefer windows to deadlines.

01

Night train from Batumi

Coast → capital · ~5 hours

Board with tea and a paperback. Wake to valley mist and the low hum of the carriage. Arrive before the city fully opens its doors—perfect for a bathhouse morning and a blank first page.

02

The Sololaki loop

On foot · half day

Climb through Art Nouveau façades, pause at courtyard gates, descend via Betlemi stairs. Count the iron balconies. Stop when someone offers coffee from a second-floor window.

03

Kakheti day rail

East to the vineyards · full day

Leave early for Signagi or Telavi country. Taste qvevri wine where it was buried. Return with a full notebook and the smell of pressed grapes in your sleeve.

Field notes

What to keep in the margins

Bathhouses

Book a private sulfur room if you want silence; shared halls if you want the city’s real soundtrack. Bring flip-flops and an empty afternoon.

Language of the table

A toast is never just a toast. Listen first. When it is your turn, speak slowly and mean it. The room will make room for you.

What not to rush

Cable-car queues, church interiors, and the last train of the evening. Some delays are the story. Leave blank lines in the notebook for them.

Packing list

Soft shoes, a foldable rain layer, a fountain pen that will not freeze on cold mornings, and enough curiosity to miss one more bus.
