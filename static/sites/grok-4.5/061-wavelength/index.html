Why sound

The ocean is never silent. That is the point.

Light fades within meters of the surface. Sound travels for kilometers, carrying the signatures of whales, reefs, ice, storms, and ships. Wavelength captures those signals as shared scientific infrastructure—not a closed product.

Open acoustic sensing means researchers, coastal managers, and educators can inspect the same raw waveforms, annotation catalogs, and calibration notes. When the ocean changes, the evidence is audible, comparable, and reusable.

  • Continuous listening

    Year-round hydrophone arrays sample the full acoustic spectrum, not just seasonal surveys.

  • Open by default

    Waveforms, metadata, and detection models ship under open licenses with clear provenance.

  • Protection-ready

    Alerts and trends map to shipping lanes, marine protected areas, and recovery plans.

How it works

From pressure wave to public dataset

Each Wavelength node is a calibrated listening post. Together they form a coherent picture of marine life and human activity across basins.

01 · Sense

Hydrophone arrays

Moored and drifting sensors capture broadband sound with clock sync, depth tags, and temperature metadata for every segment.

02 · Decode

Open detection models

Shared classifiers flag vocalizations, vessel noise, and geophony. Humans review edge cases; models improve in public repositories.

03 · Share

Research-ready archives

Spectrograms, event catalogs, and raw audio packages land in open archives with DOIs so findings can be reproduced and extended.
