Selected work

Projects that hold their ground

From harbour pavilions to courtyard housing, each commission is distilled until structure, light, and use speak in one voice.

  • 01 / 04

    Harbour Pavilion

    Copenhagen Harbour Cultural 2023

    A slender public room on the water—concrete, oak, and a continuous glass clerestory that tracks the harbour light from morning fog to evening gold.

  • 02 / 04

    North Zealand Residence

    Hornbæk Residential 2021

    Two offset volumes—one dark brick, one limewashed—frame a wind-sheltered courtyard and open the living spaces toward the Kattegat horizon.

  • 03 / 04

    Kulturhuset Extension

    Aarhus Civic 2020

    An addition that refuses to shout: a calm stone plinth and timber soffit that extend the original building’s civic rhythm into a new reading room and court.

  • 04 / 04

    Ørestad Courtyards

    Ørestad Housing 2018

    Forty-eight dwellings arranged around three planted courts—shared thresholds, private balconies, and brickwork that softens the district’s larger grain.

How we work

Discipline in service of feeling

We edit until nothing ornamental remains—then we protect what is left: proportion, material honesty, and rooms people want to inhabit.

  • 01

    Reduce, then refine

    Every project begins with subtraction. Mass, structure, and light are clarified until the plan reads as inevitable rather than arranged.

  • 02

    Materials that age

    Brick, timber, concrete, and stone are chosen for how they meet weather and use—not for first-day polish, but for the decades after.

  • 03

    Human measure

    Monumental does not mean cold. Thresholds, seats, and daylight are tuned to the body so large rooms still feel intimate.

Studio profile

A small practice with long views

Form Haus was founded in Copenhagen in 2009 by architects Freja Møller and Elias Brandt. The studio sits in a converted warehouse on Islands Brygge—close enough to the harbour that tide and weather still enter the working day.

We take on few projects at a time: civic rooms, cultural buildings, and houses that need a clear idea more than a catalogue of gestures. Collaboration with engineers, craftspeople, and clients is direct and iterative—drawings stay simple so decisions stay shared.

  • Freja Møller

    Founding partner

    Leads cultural and civic work. Former teaching fellow at the Royal Danish Academy; focuses on daylight strategy and public thresholds.

  • Elias Brandt

    Founding partner

    Oversees housing and adaptive reuse. Trained in timber construction; writes specifications that builders can actually build from.

  • Sofie Kragh

    Associate · Project lead

    Coordinates complex sites from competition through handover. Special interest in landscape interfaces and shared outdoor rooms.
